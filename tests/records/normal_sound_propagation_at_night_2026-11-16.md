## Question
Why can a distant sound sometimes be heard over a greater distance at night, especially across open ground or water?

## Explanation

On a clear, calm night, the ground can cool faster than the air above it, leaving cooler air near the surface and warmer air higher up: a temperature inversion.

Sound travels faster in warmer air. The vertical sound-speed gradient can bend sound paths downwards towards the ground. Under suitable conditions, this helps keep sound energy near the surface and detectable farther away.

Wind shear can also refract sound. Depending on its direction and vertical profile, wind may reinforce or oppose the temperature-related effect. Turbulence, atmospheric absorption, frequency and ground interaction also matter.

This is not a universal rule that “night makes sound louder”, and water itself is not a sufficient explanation. Diagnosing a particular observation requires local meteorological and acoustic conditions.
