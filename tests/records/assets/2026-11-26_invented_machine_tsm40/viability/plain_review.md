# Design Review: Sauravi TSM-40 Towable Solar Stone Mill (paper design)

Source: `spec_v.md` (Owner's Specification Sheet) only. Calculations: `/home/claude/scratch_via_plain/calc.py`.

## 1. Verdict

**Do not build as drawn. Build with changes (redesign of the energy system and several subsystems, then a prototype).**

The mill head, drive and controls are a conventional and mostly sensible layout. But the stated duty (4–7 h/day at 40–60 kg/h) needs 10–18 kWh/day. The 1.64 kWp array delivers about 6 kWh/day in good sun and about 3.5 kWh/day in the monsoon, so the machine can sustain only about 1.5–3 h/day. That is a fundamental sizing mismatch, not a tuning issue. Several other features would cause early field failures or safety hazards: battery busbar joints (aluminium studs, no locking, mixed metals) on a towed trailer; a dark, sun-exposed battery cabinet with charging allowed up to 55 °C; about 140 W of controller heat sealed in an IP65 polycarbonate box; a water-sensitive Sorel-bonded stone in a monsoon machine; and a large PV canopy whose wind uplift approaches the trailer's weight. None of these is hard to fix, but together they mean the drawing set must be revised before metal is cut.

| D16 | Mill: dry running and restart | Hopper sensor stops the motor 60 s after grain falls below it. ## 3. Calculations

All inputs come from the spec unless marked *assumed*. The script is `/home/claude/scratch_via_plain/calc.py`.

### 3.1 Electrical load
- Motor 2.2 kW S1; *assumed* motor efficiency 0.88 and controller 0.97, giving **2.58 kW electrical at full load** (2.11 kW at 1.8 kW shaft, 1.76 kW at 1.5 kW shaft).
- Cross-check from throughput: stone milling to fine flour is typically 30–50 kWh/t (*assumed* typical range). At 40–60 kg/h that is 1.2–3.0 kW shaft, so the 2.2 kW motor is consistent with the claim and will often run near full load.
- Battery current: 2.58 kW / 51.2 V = **50 A**; at 48 V (low SOC, under load) = **54 A**. Controller continuous rating is 55 A, a margin of < 5% (D08).

### 3.2 Daily energy balance (decisive)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| PV array | 4 × 410 W = 1.64 kWp |
| PV energy/day, 5 PSH, PR 0.75 (*assumed*, dry season) | 6.2 kWh |
| PV energy/day, 3 PSH (*assumed*, monsoon) | 3.7 kWh |
| Battery usable (85% of 5.4 kWh) | 4.6 kWh |
| Demand at 4 / 5.5 / 7 h, full load | 10.3 / 14.2 / 18.0 kWh |
| Sustained run time, dry season, 2.58 kW / 1.8 kW | **2.3 h / 3.3 h per day** |
| Sustained run time, monsoon | 1.4 h / 2.0 h per day |
| First day from a full battery | 4.0 h / 5.8 h |
| Annual PV ≈ 1.64 × 1,450 kWh/kWp × 0.95 (*assumed* site yield) | ≈ 2,260 kWh, which is at most ≈ 875 full-load hours/yr even if every kWh is used. Seasonal use and full-battery days reduce that further |

Result: the stated 4–7 h/day and 1,000–1,400 h/yr are not achievable. Real throughput is about 40–50% of the claim. Uncertainties are actual grinding power (measure on the prototype), site irradiance, soiling at 10° tilt, and parking orientation (the fixed tilt points toward the drawbar).

### 3.3 Storage drain
- Isolator closed: 45 mA × 24 h × 7 × (6–12 weeks) = **45–91 Ah**, against a 105 Ah pack.
- Isolator open (BMS only): 12 mA gives **12–24 Ah**, plus self-discharge (~2–3%/month, *assumed*).
- A pack parked at ≤ 25% SOC (likely, given D01) reaches UV cutoff within the storage period. With the isolator open, PV cannot recharge it.

### 3.4 Battery cabinet temperature
- Sol-air temperature = T_amb + α·G/h_o = 46 + 0.9 × 800 / 17 ≈ **88 °C** dark surface (≈ 60 °C at α = 0.3, white).
- Internal air temperature cannot be computed without louvre area and solar exposure hours. Cells in the high-40s to 50s °C on hot days are plausible, and the BMS still permits charging. Literature consistently identifies temperature, then SOC, as the dominant drivers of LFP calendar ageing; the rule of thumb is that the rate roughly doubles per +10 K (*assumed*, needs supplier data).

### 3.5 Electrical enclosure heat
- Losses: 3% × 2.58 kW + 4% × 1.64 kW ≈ **143 W** (*assumed* efficiencies).
- Enclosure size *assumed* 500 × 400 × 200 mm, A = 0.76 m², h = 5–6 W/m²K, giving ΔT ≈ **31–38 K**, so internal ≈ **77–84 °C** at 46 °C ambient.

### 3.6 Belt drive
- Centre distance from L = 1,600 mm, D = 400, d = 63: **C ≈ 401 mm**; small-pulley wrap **131.8°**.
- Belt speed 9.24 m/s; motor torque 7.5 N·m; effective pull **238 N**; T1 ≈ 348 N, T2 ≈ 110 N, static hub load ≈ 460 N.
- Ratio 6.35 gives stone speed 441 rpm and stone rim speed 9.2 m/s (consistent with the spec).
- Capacity with an *assumed* 0.25–0.31 kW/rib basic rating × 10 ribs × 0.87 (arc) × 0.95 (length) ≈ **2.1–2.6 kW**, against a required 2.2 kW × service factor 1.4–1.6 = **3.1–3.5 kW**. Undersized, pending confirmation from the belt maker's tables.
- Start-up: runner inertia ≈ 0.76 kg·m² (38 kg, r = 0.2 m, solid disc), so stored energy ≈ 0.8 kJ at 440 rpm. Reflected inertia at the motor is 0.019 kg·m², so a 4 s ramp needs only ≈ 1.4 N·m. Empty start-up is not an issue; restart with grain packed in the gap is (D16).

### 3.7 Wind on the canopy
- Canopy ≈ 4 × 1.95 m² = 7.8 m² (*assumed* module size 1.72 × 1.13 m).
- q = ½ρV²: 391 Pa at 25 m/s, 562 Pa at 30 m/s. Net uplift with Cf ≈ 1.3 (*assumed*, monopitch canopy with partial blockage) = **4.0–5.7 kN**, against a trailer weight of **6.3 kN**. The overturning margin is very small once eccentricity is included.

### 3.8 Cables, fits, misc.
- Motor phase rms ≈ 52 A × √(2/3) ≈ **42 A**, against 6 mm² H07RN-F derated to ≈ **30 A** at 46 °C.
- Al housing on an 80 mm bearing OD: (23 − 11.5) × 10⁻⁶ × 80 mm × 40 K ≈ **37 µm** extra clearance.
- Hopper run-on: 60 s × 50 kg/h ≈ **0.83 kg** drawn after the level sensor clears. The grain held below the sensor is unknown (needs hopper drawing); probably less than this.
- Feed cam: 6 lobes × 440 rpm = 44 Hz, so ≈ **1.9 × 10⁸ cycles/yr**.
- PV: Voc at 10 °C ≈ 103 V (< 150 V MPPT limit, OK). Hot Vmp ≈ 70 V, still above the 58.4 V charge voltage, OK. Charge current 1.64 kW / 52 V ≈ 31 A (MPPT 60 A, OK). 1.5 × Isc ≈ 15.8 A > 15 A fuse.
- Bearings by load alone: motor 6205 (C ≈ 14 kN) at ~0.7 kN hub load gives L10 ≈ 47,000 h, OK. The spindle bearings are lightly loaded. Their life will be set by contamination, fit and lubrication, not rating.

### 3.9 Remaining uncertainties
Actual grinding power and specific energy (dominant); site irradiance and soiling; real cabinet/enclosure temperatures; belt maker ratings; Sorel-bond wet strength; stone wear rate; trailer geometry (nose weight, CG height, canopy height); BMS behaviour after UV cutoff; and whether the MPPT can wake a BMS-isolated pack.

## 4. Prototype tests (before production)

1. **Energy and throughput**: measure kW, kWh/kg and kg/h on maize at fine/medium settings, and on other cereals/pulses. Run multi-day field logging (PV, SOC, run hours) in both dry and monsoon seasons to confirm the achievable h/day.
2. **Thermal**: thermocouple the cells (all four corners, centre), busbars, cabinet air, enclosure air, controller heatsinks, motor, belt and spindle bearings during a 46 °C-class hot-day full-load run with sun on the cabinet. Include a clogged-filter case.
3. **Road/vibration endurance**: e.g. 2,000+ km equivalent on unpaved test track (or shaker profile). Afterwards check busbar torque/resistance (milliohm per joint), terminal and lug security, PV clamp torque, EL imaging of the modules for micro-cracks, false brinelling on the spindle bearings, motor plate pivot wear, stone chipping and canopy weld cracks.
4. **Battery and BMS**: storage test (12 weeks, isolator open/closed, low starting SOC). UV/OV/OT cutoff and recovery behaviour. Charger wake-up after a BMS cutoff. SOC drift over 30 cycles. Balancing effectiveness. Pre-charge/inrush on isolator closing.
5. **Mill function**: gap repeatability and parallelism hot and cold; tramp relief with a steel nut and a stone; dry-run duration at hopper empty; restart with a packed gap after an E-stop or UV cutoff; flour temperature and fineness distribution; upper-bearing housing fit after thermal cycling.
6. **Stone durability and food safety**: water immersion and humidity soak of the bond (RH 95%, 40 °C, 6–12 weeks); wear rate (mm per tonne) and furrow life; flour analysis for ash, silica, Mg/Cl and polymer contamination against applicable food standards.
7. **Environmental ingress**: rain/spray test of the whole trailer (stone housing, enclosure, cabinet louvres, motor); enclosure condensation over day/night humidity cycles; dust test of the filters, motor fins and bearing seals.
8. **Electrical safety and EMC**: PV insulation resistance wet and dry at dawn; earth-bond continuity of frames/rails after corrosion exposure (salt-fog/humidity cycling of a mounting sample); surge immunity; Hall-signal integrity at full PWM load; cable temperature rise at full current.
9. **Belt drive**: slip and temperature at full and overload torque; tension over the NEW–END swing; start-up behaviour and plate oscillation; belt life accelerated test.
10. **Structural and road**: canopy and clamp wind-load verification (static load test to the design wind); parked overturning/anchor test; towing stability (crosswind, 40 km/h, sway); nose weight; brake and lighting compliance with local trailer regulations.
11. **Usability and maintenance**: lubrication, belt change, stone dressing, filter cleaning and transport-lock procedures done by a representative operator with field tools.
