# TSM-40 Towable Solar Stone Mill — Design Viability Review

Basis: owner's specification sheet (spec_v) only. The machine has not been built. Figures marked "typical" are general engineering values, not measurements of this machine.

## 1. Verdict

**Do not build (as drawn). Redesign and re-review before a prototype.**

The energy budget does not close. A 1.64 kWp array on the canopy gives roughly 3.7–6.8 kWh/day. Running the mill near its 2.2 kW rating for the stated 4–7 h/day needs about 10–18 kWh/day. On consecutive working days that supports only about 1.5–3 h/day (roughly 70–190 kg of flour), against the 160–420 kg/day the duty cycle implies. The canopy area limits how much PV can be added, so this is a sizing problem, not something a detail change fixes. Several other high-severity faults point the same way. The battery and electronics will run hot. The battery's busbar joints are likely to work loose and corrode. The stones have 3 mm of furrow for 30 mm of wear allowance, and their bond softens in water. Flour temperatures will reach the trip point in hot weather. The guarding and stopping of a 1 kJ runner stone are not adequate. The mechanical concept is sound, but it needs a revised duty claim or power system, a redesigned enclosure and battery package, and fixes to the stones, transport and safety before a first build is worth testing.

Hard to see before it fails | Medium | Cast-iron or steel housing, or a steel sleeve insert, with anti-creep retention. Add a labyrinth or purged seal ahead of the 2RS. ## 3. Calculations

All calculations are rough. Inputs are from the spec unless marked as typical figures.

### 3.1 Energy budget (decides viability)

| Item | Input | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Battery draw at full load | 2.2 kW shaft ÷ 0.88 motor ÷ 0.97 controller | 2.58 kW → 50 A at 51.2 V, 54 A at 48 V |
| Battery draw, lighter load | 1.5–2.0 kW shaft | 1.76–2.34 kW |
| Daily demand | 1.76–2.58 kW × 4–7 h | 7.0–18.0 kWh/day |
| Specific energy implied | 2.58 kW ÷ 40–60 kg/h | 43–65 kWh/t (plausible for fine stone grinding; not verified) |
| PV energy per day | 1.64 kWp × 3.0 / 4.5 / 5.5 peak-sun-hours × 0.75 performance ratio (typical; includes heat, wiring, MPPT, dust) | 3.7 / 5.5 / 6.8 kWh/day |
| Usable battery | 5.4 kWh × 0.9 | 4.9 kWh (covers about 1.9–2.8 h at load, once) |
| Hours/day sustainable over a multi-day run | PV ÷ load | 1.4–3.8 h/day (2.2–2.6 h at 2.3 kW and 4.5 PSH) |
| Flour/day sustainable at 50 kg/h | | about 70–190 kg/day against 160–420 kg/day claimed |

Uncertain: the real shaft power at 40–60 kg/h (needs a dynamometer or current log). Local peak-sun-hours in harvest seasons. Soiling from the mill's own dust. Shading by the canopy legs and surroundings. Even at the most favourable combination (1.5 kW shaft, 5.5 PSH), the result is under 4 h/day.

### 3.2 Battery storage drain

| Drain | 6 weeks | 12 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| 12 mA (BMS standby) | 12 Ah = 12% | 24 Ah = 23% |
| 45 mA (isolator closed, no PV) | 45 Ah = 43% | 91 Ah = 86% |

Add self-discharge of about 2–3%/month (typical). A pack stored at 30% charge for 12 weeks with the isolator open reaches about 0–5%.

### 3.3 Battery joints

At a joint resistance of 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 mΩ and 52 A: 0.27 / 1.35 / 2.7 W of heat per joint, and a sense-voltage error of 5 / 26 / 52 mV. A 52 mV error matters on LFP's flat curve and near the 2.60 V cutoff.

### 3.4 Electrical enclosure heat

Assumed enclosure 400 × 300 × 200 mm (dimensions not given): 0.52 m². Overall heat-transfer coefficient about 5.5 W/m²K (typical for a sealed plastic enclosure). Losses of 60 / 100 / 140 W give an internal rise of 21 / 35 / 49 K, so 67–95 °C inside at 46 °C ambient. Uncertain: real controller efficiencies and the enclosure size.

### 3.5 Belt drive

- Centre distance from belt length 1,600 mm and pulleys 63 / 400 mm: about 401 mm. Small-pulley wrap about 130°.
- Belt speed: π × 0.063 m × 2,800 / 60 = 9.24 m/s. Effective tension at 2.2 kW: 238 N. At a 70 A start peak: about 330 N.
- Assuming an effective poly-V friction of 0.5–0.7: tension ratio 3.1–4.9, tight side 300–350 N, slack side 60–110 N, hub load about 360–460 N.
- Uncertain: the motor and plate mass and the hinge geometry, so it is not known whether gravity alone supplies the needed slack-side tension. Also not known whether torque reaction adds to or subtracts from it.

### 3.6 Flour temperature rise

ΔT = heat fraction × P × 3600 ÷ (kg/h × 1.6 kJ/kg·K). With 30–50% of 1.5–2.2 kW going into 40–60 kg/h: 17–62 K. With grain at 35–46 °C, the flour exits above the 55 °C trip under most of this range. Uncertain: how the heat splits between flour, stones, housing and air. Measure it on the prototype.

### 3.7 Stone life

The wear allowance is 30 mm per stone, but the furrows are only 3 mm deep. Without re-dressing, the grinding faces lose their geometry after about 10% of the nominal wear allowance. The wear rate per tonne is unknown.

### 3.8 Spindle thermal effect on gap

Steel expands about 12 µm/m·K. A 300–500 mm bearing span with a 10–20 K difference changes by 0.036–0.12 mm, which is 0.7–2.4 dial divisions (0.05 mm each). Uncertain: the real span and the temperature distribution.

### 3.9 Stone energy and speeds

Runner inertia is estimated at about 0.97 kg·m² (25 kg stone disc + 13 kg ring and hub near the rim; the split is assumed). Stored energy at 440 rpm is about 1.0 kJ. Rim speed is 9.2 m/s, inside the normal range for small stone mills.

### 3.10 Agitator cycles

6 lobes × 440 rpm × 60 × 1,200 h = 1.9×10⁸ strikes per year.

### 3.11 Checks that pass

- PV voltage: Voc at 10 °C ≈ 99 × (1 + 0.0027 × 15) ≈ 103 V, below the MPPT's 150 V limit. Vmp at 70 °C cell temperature ≈ 70 V, above the battery charge voltage (58.4 V).
- MPPT current: about 31 A at full array power, well under 60 A.
- Battery C-rate: about 0.5C discharge and 0.3C charge, acceptable for LFP.
- 125 A Class-T fuse with 35 mm² cable: appropriate.
- Stone rim speed: acceptable.

## 4. Prototype tests (before production)

1. **Energy balance in the field.** Log PV energy, battery energy, motor input power and kg of flour over at least 10 consecutive working days in each season (hot pre-monsoon harvest, monsoon, cool season). Measure kWh/t at the claimed fineness. Pass condition: the stated daily hours are achieved without the charge level trending down.
2. **Thermal survey at 46 °C (sun plus heat chamber).** Thermocouples on edge and centre cells, cabinet air, enclosure air, controller heatsinks, motor, both spindle bearings and the flour. Run at full load from 10:00 to 16:00. Record any derating, charge inhibit or trips.
3. **Flour quality and temperature** across the gap range and with grain at 25 and 45 °C. Measure trip frequency and particle size. Check fineness drift over a 3 h run (gap change as the spindle warms).
4. **Road durability.** At least 2,000 km on a representative unpaved route at up to 40 km/h, with accelerometers on the canopy, motor plate, battery and mill head. Afterwards: EL imaging of the modules, battery joint resistance and torque check, bearing inspection for false brinelling, belt and motor-cable inspection, enclosure terminal pull test.
5. **Battery joint ageing.** Thermal cycling plus humidity (85% RH) plus vibration on a pack section. Track joint micro-ohm resistance and nut torque retention.
6. **Storage test.** Park for 12 weeks with the isolator open and with it closed (shaded), starting from 30% charge. Track cell voltages and confirm the BMS cuts off before damage.
7. **Moisture.** Spray/rain test and 2-week monsoon-humidity soak with day/night cycling. Check for condensation inside the enclosure and cabinet, and measure PV insulation resistance at morning wake-up.
8. **Stone durability.** Wet-dry cycling and humid storage of stone samples. Measure strength retention and grit loss. Test flour for ash or grit and chloride. Track furrow depth against tonnes milled, and validate the dressing procedure.
9. **Belt drive.** Start under full load (hopper full, gap set fine) 500 times. Check slip from motor speed against the stone-speed sensor. Look for plate lift and pulley/key fretting. Repeat with a worn (stretched) belt at the END mark.
10. **Wear and contamination.** 500 h endurance run. Measure wear of the agitator finger, cam and bushes, and detect plastic or metal in the flour. Run a foreign-object test (steel nut) to confirm the bed-stone relief works and to judge stone damage.
11. **Safety.** Measure run-down time. Verify the lid interlock and guard locking, the hard-wired emergency stop with the controller faulted, and that the belt guard is complete. Electrical insulation and earthing tests to the applicable machinery and low-voltage DC standards.
12. **Towing stability.** Measure tongue weight, check sway at 40 km/h in crosswind, and verify canopy strength against the design wind.
