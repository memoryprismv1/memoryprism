# Sauravi TSM-40 Towable Solar Stone Mill — Maintenance Checklist

Based only on the owner's specification sheet. Hour intervals assume the stated duty of about 1,000–1,400 h/year, so 250 h is roughly one month of season use. Where an interval is given as "X h or calendar time", use whichever comes first. Tighten every fastener to the part maker's value. The torque figures below are only typical values.

## 1. Checklist

| ID | Part / subsystem | Task | Interval | Reason (wear / failure mechanism) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Trailer set-up at site | Inspect / adjust: park level, chock wheels, park so the battery cabinet is shaded in the afternoon if possible | Each use | A vertical-axis mill that is not level feeds unevenly and wears the stones on one side. The dark steel battery cabinet heats in sun, and LiFePO4 ages much faster above about 40 °C. |
| C02 | Grain input | Test moisture (meter, ≤14% w.b.); sieve or winnow out stones, sand and non-ferrous debris | Each batch | Damp grain glazes and clogs the stones, raises flour temperature, and brings water into the Sorel-cement bond, which water softens. The magnet does not catch stones or non-ferrous objects. |
| C03 | Hopper throat magnet | Clean: remove the tramp iron it has collected | Each use (and mid-day when grain is dirty) | A loaded magnet stops catching iron. Iron that reaches the stones chips the faces and furrows and lifts the bed stone against its relief springs. |
| C04 | Feed agitator wire finger and 6-lobe UHMW-PE cam | Inspect for cracks or bending in the finger and worn lobes on the cam | Each use; replace finger at first crack, cam when lobes are visibly flattened | The spring-steel finger bends six times per stone revolution (about 2,600 times a minute), so it is a fatigue part. It sits **below** the magnet, so a broken piece falls into the stone eye and into the flour. A worn cam shakes less, so feed rate drops. |
| C05 | Stone housing, sweeper path, outlet chute, hopper | Clean dry with brush or vacuum. Remove all flour and grain residue | End of each day | Old flour in humid weather goes mouldy or rancid and draws insects and rodents (a food-safety problem). Caked flour can block the outlet and hold heat. Never use water (see Do-not list). |
| C06 | Motor fan inlet, cowl and cooling fins | Clean flour and mineral dust off | Daily in the dust season; weekly otherwise | The TEFC motor sheds heat only through its fins. Dust insulates them, so the motor runs hotter, which ages the bearing grease and windings. The winding NTC only derates at 110 °C, long after damage has started. |
| C07 | Display: running values | Inspect: before the start, SOC, battery V and no fault codes. Under load, stone rpm close to 440 and flour temperature below about 45 °C | Each use (note in log) | The stone-speed trip acts only below 250 rpm. A slipping belt at 300–400 rpm is not tripped but glazes and heats the belt. The 55 °C flour trip is too late for flour quality. |
| C08 | Spindle bearing housings (upper and lower), motor end shields | Inspect by hand or with an IR thermometer, and listen for rumble or squeal | About 1 h into each day's run | No sensor watches bearing temperature or vibration. A rising temperature or new noise is the only early warning of failure. |
| C09 | Stone gap control | Adjust: start with the gap open, close it once grain is flowing, open it before the hopper runs empty and before STOP | Each run | The hopper sensor lets the motor run for 60 s after grain falls below it. Stones touching with no grain rub, which wears and glazes them, overheats them and puts grit into the flour. |
| C10 | Hopper level sensor | Clean the sensor face. Watch for grain bridging above the throat | Daily | Dust or damp film on the face gives false "full" readings. If grain bridges above the throat, the sensor still sees grain while the stones run empty. |
| C11 | Emergency stop and START/STOP | Test: press the e-stop while running and confirm the stop and the latch | Weekly | It is a safety device and nothing else tests it. Vibration and dust can stop the contacts working. |
| C12 | Belt tension pointer and motor hinge | Inspect: record the pointer position on the NEW–END scale. Lift the motor plate slightly by hand and let it drop, and confirm it swings freely and settles back | Weekly | Tension comes only from the plate's weight. If the bronze bushes seize (flour dust soaking up the oil, corrosion), the belt loses tension and slips, and the pointer gives a false reading. A pointer that moves suddenly means the belt is stretching or damaged. |
| C13 | PV array glass | Clean with a soft brush and low-mineral water, early morning or evening when the glass is cool | Weekly in the dry season; as needed otherwise (when PV power drops on clear days) | At a 10° tilt, rain does not wash off dust well, and soiling cuts charging. The anti-reflective coating scratches easily, and cold water on hot glass risks thermal shock. |
| C14 | Battery cabinet louvre filter mats | Clean (wash, dry **fully** before refitting); replace if torn or felted | Inspect weekly in the dust season; wash when visibly loaded; replace each year | Ventilation is passive and has no fan. Blocked mats push cell temperature up, and the BMS allows charging up to 55 °C, so heat damage builds up with no warning. A wet mat lets humid air in. |
| C15 | Electrical enclosure: exterior, ePTFE vent, door gasket | Inspect and clean: wipe dust off the vent. Open the door and look for condensation, water or corrosion | Monthly; weekly during the monsoon | The vent is on the **top face**, where water and dust settle on the membrane. The enclosure has no drain, so day–night breathing in high humidity builds up condensate inside, around the controllers and terminal blocks. |
| C16 | Lower spindle bearing (30208) | Lubricate: wipe the nipple, then put in **2–3 g** (about 2 slow strokes of a hand gun) of the same NSF H1 lithium-complex NLGI 2 grease, with the spindle turning slowly or turned by hand | Every 250 h | Re-greasing keeps the bearing supplied and helps keep dust out of the seals. With **no relief port**, extra grease has nowhere to go: it pushes out the lip seals or churns and overheats. Stop if you feel resistance. Make up for the missing purge with the yearly repack (C34). |
| C17 | Poly-V belt and pulley grooves | Inspect for cracks across the ribs, missing or torn ribs, glazing, edge fraying and dust packed in the grooves. Clean the grooves dry | Every 250 h | EPDM belts wear by rib abrasion (dust and grit speed this up) and by slip glazing. Packed grooves raise the belt, so it slips. With a 63 mm motor pulley and a 6.35:1 ratio, the belt wraps only a short arc of the small pulley, so it is prone to slip. |
| C18 | Pulley fastenings | Inspect / adjust: motor pulley M8 set screw (check the key is not walking out), taper-lock grub screws, four target bolt heads on the stone pulley, proximity sensor gap | At 20–50 h after any fitting or belt change, then every 250 h and after each tow | A plain-bore pulley held by one set screw loosens under start torque and vibration, wearing the shaft and key. A loose taper-lock bush wobbles. A missing target bolt makes the rpm read 25% low and can cause false trips. |
| C19 | Motor hinge pin, sintered bronze bushes | Lubricate with a few drops of food-grade (H1) light oil, not grease. Check for play | Every 250 h (more often in the dust season); replace bushes when the plate can be rocked sideways | The pin rocks through small angles all the time from belt pulses and towing. That wears out the oil in the sintered bronze, and flour dust soaks it up. A worn bush lets the motor pulley tilt out of line, and a dry bush seizes (see C12). |
| C20 | Motor phase and Hall/thermistor cable (300 mm free loop) | Inspect: sheath cracking, kinks, the gland on the motor box, the P-clip. Check for chafing | Every 250 h and after each tow | The loop flexes every time the plate swings, from towing vibration and from belt stretch over the 16° range. The 0.5 mm² Hall and thermistor cores break first, causing intermittent commutation faults, stall trips or loss of over-temperature protection. |
| C21 | Grinding stones (pair) | Inspect / measure: thickness against the 30 mm groove, furrow depth (new 3 mm), cracks in the stone, cement backing and steel ring, loose ring. Re-zero the gap dial at the touch point | Every 250 h | Stones wear thinner, and the furrows wear shallow. Shallow furrows cut throughput and raise flour temperature. Dress or replace as a matched pair. As the stones wear, the dial zero drifts, so dial settings stop matching the real gap. A cracked bond or a loose ring makes the stone unbalanced and can break it up. |
| C22 | Gap mechanism: M24 trapezoidal screw, 3 guide posts, relief springs | Clean. Lubricate the screw and posts lightly with H1 grease, wiping off the excess. Check the springs for corrosion or set and the posts for free lift | Every 250 h | If the posts stick from dust paste or corrosion, the 6 mm relief stops working and a hard object damages the stones or the spindle bearing. Zinc-plated springs corrode in the monsoon and lose force. |
| C23 | Feed spout pin and UHMW-PE bushes | Inspect wear and play. Replace the bushes when the spout rattles or sits off-centre | Every 250 h; expect yearly replacement | The bushes run dry in the grain stream, so grain dust wears them away. A sloppy spout feeds the stones off-centre, and the worn plastic ends up in the flour. |
| C24 | Sweeper rubber tips | Inspect for wear or tears. Replace if worn | Every 250 h | Worn tips leave flour circling in the drum, which builds heat, lowers output and leaves residue. |
| C25 | Battery: cell balance and SOC calibration | Test: read all 16 cell voltages from the BMS data (not just pack V), at rest and near full charge. Let the pack reach full charge (all cells above 3.40 V) for several hours | Monthly; plus a full charge at least monthly in season | Balancing works only above 3.40 V/cell and only at 60 mA, and the mill drains the battery daily. A pack that rarely reaches full charge drifts out of balance, and its coulomb-counted SOC drifts. The display shows only pack voltage and SOC. A weak or out-of-balance cell is invisible until the BMS cuts off. |
| C26 | Battery cell busbars and terminal nuts (Al M6 studs, steel nuts, Cu busbars) | Inspect: IR thermometer scan of every joint under milling load, look for discolouration or oxidation. Adjust: re-torque to the cell maker's value (typically about 4–6 N·m; do not exceed) | IR scan monthly. Re-torque at 50 h, then every 500 h or quarterly, and after rough tows | There are no spring washers. Towing vibration, heat cycling and creep of the aluminium studs loosen the nuts, and steel against aluminium corrodes in humid air. A loose joint at about 50 A overheats locally. The single NTC on cell 8 will not see a hot joint on another cell. |
| C27 | Battery cabinet internal temperature | Test: measure air and cell-surface temperatures (end cells and the sun-facing side) on a hot afternoon | Monthly in the hot season | The only cell sensor is in the pack centre, but the sun heats the cabinet walls, so edge cells near the sunlit side can be hotter than cell 8. No cabinet air sensor is fitted. |
| C28 | Electrical enclosure interior | Adjust: re-torque the M6 brass power studs (battery and motor phases). Retighten the rising-cage signal terminals. Fit bootlace ferrules on fine-stranded conductors. Inspect for dust, corrosion, heat discolouration and gasket compression set | At 50 h, then every 500 h or quarterly | Vibration from the mill and from towing loosens the lugs, and a loose lug at 55–70 A overheats. Fine strands without ferrules spread out and settle under the clamp, giving intermittent Hall, speed or thermistor signals and stray trips. Brass studs shear if over-tightened. |
| C29 | PV mounting (Al frames / A2 clamps / galvanized rails) | Inspect for galvanic corrosion: white powder or pitting on the aluminium frames, zinc loss or red rust on the rails around the clamps. Re-torque the M8 clamps. Test bonding continuity from each panel frame to the trailer frame and earth bar (low ohms) | Every 500 h or quarterly; after each monsoon | Aluminium, stainless and zinc touch with no isolation, so in rain and humidity the aluminium and zinc corrode. The clamps loosen, which with towing can let a panel move or come off. A corroded frame bond means a PV-to-panel-frame fault no longer shows in the MPPT's PV-to-frame insulation test, so the panel frame could become live. |
| C30 | PV wiring: MC4 connectors, 4 and 6 mm² cables, nylon ties, homerun gland | Inspect that connectors are fully latched and not heat-marked. Check for cable chafe at canopy legs and edges, brittle or broken ties, a drip loop before the gland, gland tightness | Every 500 h or quarterly and after each tow; replace ties yearly | Towing vibration works connectors loose (high resistance, heat, arcing). Even UV-stabilized nylon ties go brittle at deck temperatures above 55 °C and in UV. A loose cable rubs through its insulation, which the MPPT reports only as an insulation fault the next morning. |
| C31 | Main DC circuit: Class-T fuse, 35 mm² crimped lugs, 63 A rotary isolator | Inspect for discolouration, loose lugs, damaged crimps. IR-scan the isolator and fuse holder under load | Every 500 h or quarterly | The isolator runs close to its rating (motor about 45–55 A continuous, 70 A peak, plus charging current). Worn contacts or loose lugs heat up, and nothing monitors this. |
| C32 | Spindle bearings: play and preload | Test: with a dial indicator at the runner rim, check radial and axial play. Check the upper bearing for outer-ring creep (fretting dust or polish in the aluminium housing). Check the 30208 locknut and tab washer | Every 500 h and after any hard-object event | Aluminium expands more than the steel ring as it warms, so the H7 bore can loosen and let the outer ring creep and wear the housing. Lost preload on the tapered bearing makes the runner wobble, so the gap is uneven and the stones may touch. |
| C33 | Belt alignment | Test: straightedge or laser across both pulleys, at the current pointer position | At each belt change, every 500 h, and when the pointer passes about 75% toward END | The motor pulley moves along an arc as the plate swings, and hinge wear adds tilt. Poly-V belts accept only small misalignment (about ½°), beyond which the ribs wear, the belt rides out of the grooves or comes off. |
| C34 | Lower spindle bearing: overhaul | Replace the lip seals. Clean out old grease, inspect the rollers and cups, repack about ⅓–½ full with H1 grease, reset preload | Every 1,000–1,400 h (yearly, before season) | With no relief port, old grease and dust are never pushed out. The double nitrile lips wear in dust. |
| C35 | Upper spindle bearing (6208-2RS) | Replace (bearing and circlip). Check housing bore size | About 3,000 h (every 2 seasons), or sooner on noise, heat or play | It is sealed for life but sits in the flour and corundum-grit dust zone, 150 mm below hot stones. Abrasive dust wears the seals, grit gets in, and the bearing fails. It cannot be re-greased. |
| C36 | Motor bearings (6205-2RS) | Inspect by listening and checking for play. Replace | Inspect every 500 h; replace on noise or play, or at about 10,000 h | The belt pulls sideways on an overhung pulley, so the drive-end bearing carries the most load. Grease life shortens in heat and dust. |
| C37 | Poly-V belt | Replace | When the pointer reaches END, on any crack, glazing or rib damage, or at about 3,000 h / 3 years | EPDM wears and stretches. Near END, gravity tension also changes with the plate angle. |
| C38 | ePTFE vent and enclosure door gasket | Replace the vent yearly (or when blocked or torn). Replace the gasket when flattened, cracked or hardened | Vent yearly; gasket every 2–3 years | A blocked membrane stops pressure equalising, so each temperature change draws humid air past the gasket. Neoprene takes a compression set and ages in ozone and heat. |
| C39 | Battery pack: capacity and condition | Test: full charge, then discharge measured over a working day, and compare against 105 Ah. Inspect cells for swelling or venting, cell restraint and hold-down tightness, cabinet corrosion, door seal | Yearly, before season | This shows capacity fade and identifies a weak cell. Prismatic cells need restraint, and towing shakes them. |
| C40 | Mill frame rubber mounts and frame bolts; canopy legs and welds | Inspect rubber for cracking or settling, bolt tightness, cracks at canopy-leg welds, white rust on galvanizing, paint damage | Every 500 h / quarterly and after rough tows | The canopy and PV array sit high on legs and sway with every tow, a fatigue load on the welds. Rubber ages in heat and ozone. The painted mild-steel frame rusts in the monsoon. |
| C41 | Trailer running gear | Inspect tyre pressure and condition (each tow), wheel-nut torque, wheel-bearing play, leaf springs, U-bolts and shackles, coupling, safety chain, lights. Repack wheel bearings yearly. Replace tyres at about 6 years of age even if tread remains | Tyres and lights before each tow; bearings and springs every 3 months; repack yearly | Unpaved roads at 40 km/h with 640 kg load the trailer hard. Tyres crack in sun and while stored. |
| C42 | Before each tow | Adjust: open the stone gap fully (stones well apart), empty the hopper and close the gate, latch the lid, open the isolator. Support the motor plate with a block or strap so it cannot bounce, and tag it "REMOVE BEFORE START". Secure the cables | Before each tow | The runner stone (38 kg) has no spindle lock, so road shocks hammer the stone faces and both bearings. A free-hanging motor bounces on the belt, which loads the hinge bushes, the belt and the free cable loop and can throw the belt off. |
| C43 | After each tow | Inspect / test: remove the motor-plate support, check the belt is seated and the pointer has returned to its last reading. Turn the spindle slowly by hand and feel for roughness or notchiness. Check PV clamps and MC4s, pulley fasteners, cable loop. Run briefly empty with the gap open before grinding | After each tow | Vibration on a stationary bearing dents the races (false brinelling). Fasteners and connectors loosen. |
| C44 | Before storage (6–12 weeks) | Clean every trace of grain and flour. Dry the stones, open the gap, close the lid. Put the battery at about 50–60% SOC and **open the isolator**. Unload the belt by supporting the motor plate. Cover with a breathable cover, never airtight. Rodent-proof the cable entries. Raise the trailer on stands or inflate the tyres fully | Before storage | With the isolator closed the system draws 45 mA (about 1% SOC/day), which can drain a pack stored at low SOC in 2–3 weeks. Even with it open, the BMS draws 12 mA (about 23% over 12 weeks). Left at 100% in heat, the battery ages faster. Residue draws rodents (which chew cables) and mould. An airtight cover traps condensation on the water-sensitive stones. |
| C45 | During storage | Test: pack SOC and voltage. Recharge to about 60% if below about 30%. Turn the spindle and motor by hand a few turns. Check for water, nests and pests | Every 4 weeks | This stops the BMS's own drain from deep-discharging the pack (the 2.60 V cut-off disconnects the load but not the BMS). Turning redistributes grease and stops rollers resting in one spot. |
| C46 | After storage / start of season | Inspect for rodent damage to cables, nests in the motor cowl, louvres and hopper, condensation in the enclosure. Read cell voltages, then full-charge to balance. Do C16, C21, C29, C41. Confirm no PV insulation fault at morning wake-up. Run empty briefly with the gap open, then a test batch | Before first use after storage | Problems that grew during storage show up here, before load is applied. |

## 2. Do not

- **Do not let water reach the stones**: no washing, hosing, wet cloth or steam, and no grain above 14% moisture. The magnesium oxychloride bond is non-hydraulic and softens and leaches when wet. If the stones get wet, let them dry completely and check the bond before use.
- **Do not run the stones touching with no grain, or start with the gap closed.** Dry contact glazes and wears the faces, overheats them and puts grit in the flour. The 60 s hopper delay is enough to cause this.
- **Do not tow with the gap closed, grain in the hopper, or the motor plate hanging free.** The stones hammer together, the spindle bearings get dented, and the belt and hinge take shock loads.
- **Do not put belt dressing, oil or grease on the belt or pulley grooves, and do not add a tensioning screw or extra weight to the motor plate.** EPDM swells and slips with oil, and the drive is designed for gravity tension.
- **Do not grease the sintered bronze hinge bushes** (grease clogs the oil pores; use a few drops of light H1 oil), **and do not lubricate the UHMW-PE feed-spout bushes or cam.** They run dry in the food path, and lubricant collects grain dust into an abrasive paste.
- **Do not over-grease the lower spindle bearing or use a powered grease gun.** There is no relief port, so the grease pushes out the lip seals and the bearing churns and overheats. Do not mix in a different grease type or a non-food-grade grease.
- **Do not over-tighten the aluminium cell studs or the brass controller studs, or use impact tools on them.** They strip or shear. A stripped cell terminal ruins the cell.
- **Do not operate the DC isolator, pull MC4 connectors or remove gPV fuses under load.** DC arcs. Stop the motor first. The PV array stays live (about 99 V open circuit) whenever light falls on it, even with the isolator open. Cover the panels before working on PV wiring.
- **Do not rewire the array to more than 2 panels in series.** Voc would exceed the MPPT's 150 V limit.
- **Do not pressure-wash, scrape or use abrasive or solvent cleaners on the panels, splash cold water on hot glass, or stand or lean on the panels.** These damage the anti-reflective coating and can crack the glass or cells.
- **Do not store the machine with a low battery, with the isolator closed out of sun, or at 100% SOC in heat.** These cause deep discharge or accelerated ageing.
- **Do not block or seal the battery louvres, or paint the vent membrane or cover it with tape.** That causes overheating, condensation or pressure stress on the gasket. If you shield the low front louvre from road spray while towing, remove the shield before charging or running.
- **Do not wrap the machine in an airtight tarp.** It traps condensation on the stones, electronics and cell terminals.
- **Do not fit a single replacement cell without matching its capacity and voltage to the pack (top-balance it first).** The 60 mA balancer cannot correct a large mismatch.
- **Do not weld on the trailer with the battery and controllers connected.** Welding current through the frame and signal wiring destroys the BMS and controllers.
- **Do not bypass the hopper sensor, the flour or winding temperature trips, or the e-stop, and do not run with the hopper magnet removed.**
- **Do not shorten the 300 mm free loop of the motor cable or move its P-clip.** The loop has to flex through the full 16° plate swing.

## 3. What the machine's own sensors and protections do NOT cover (manual checks needed)

1. **Spindle and motor bearing condition**: no temperature or vibration sensing (C08, C32, C35, C36). The stall and speed trips act only after a seizure.
2. **Belt slip, wear and alignment**: the speed trip acts only below 250 rpm. Partial slip, glazing, cracking and misalignment are invisible (C07, C17, C33). The NEW/END pointer reads true only if the hinge swings freely (C12).
3. **Battery cell temperatures other than cell 8, cabinet air temperature, and hot busbar joints** (C26, C27). Charging is still allowed up to 55 °C, so heat ageing is never flagged.
4. **Cell imbalance and SOC drift**: the display shows only pack voltage and a coulomb-counted SOC. Cells are balanced only at full charge and slowly (C25, C39). Cell swelling and restraint are not sensed.
5. **Parasitic drain in storage**: the BMS's own 12 mA draw continues below its 2.60 V cut-off (C44, C45).
6. **Electrical enclosure interior**: temperature, condensation, water ingress, loose power lugs and loose signal terminals are not sensed (C15, C28).
7. **PV mounting corrosion and frame bonding**: the insulation test measures only to the trailer frame, once each morning. It does not show galvanic corrosion, loose clamps, a lost panel-frame bond, hot MC4 connectors, cracked cells or soiling (beyond lower PV power) (C13, C29, C30).
8. **Main DC fuse, isolator and lug heating** (C31).
9. **Stone wear, furrow depth, cracks, ring or backing damage and dial-zero drift** (C21). Stones touching while running empty are not detected (bridging, 60 s hopper delay).
10. **Foreign objects downstream of the magnet or non-ferrous ones**: a broken agitator finger, stones, sand (C02, C04). Grain moisture is not measured (C02).
11. **Motor cooling blockage** (seen only late, as winding derating) and **Hall/thermistor cable fatigue** (seen only as nuisance trips) (C06, C20).
12. **Loose mechanical fasteners**: pulley set screw and key, taper-lock, runner hub nut, gap-mechanism sticking, relief-spring condition (C18, C22).
13. **E-stop function itself**: it is never self-tested (C11).
14. **Trailer and structure**: tyres, wheel bearings, springs, coupling, lights, canopy welds, frame mounts (C40, C41).
15. **Food hygiene**: flour residue, mould, insects and rodents (C05, C44).
