# Sauravi TSM-40 Towable Solar Stone Mill: Maintenance Checklist

Derived only from the owner's specification sheet, plus general engineering facts about the standard parts it names. No manual exists for this machine. Every interval below is a starting value: shorten it if inspections find wear, and lengthen it only once records show the part is doing better than expected.

Scale assumed: 1,000 to 1,400 h/yr, so about 100 to 130 h per month in season; towed 1 to 4 times a month; laid up for 6 to 12 weeks between seasons. "Each use" means each operating day.

## 1. Checklist

| ID | Part / subsystem | Task | Interval | Reason (wear / failure mechanism) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Hopper plate magnet | Clean: open the gate, wipe tramp iron off the magnet face | Each use (before filling) | A magnet covered in iron catches less. Tramp iron that gets past it chips the stones and loads the spindle bearings. The magnet does nothing for parts that fail downstream of it (see C14). |
| C02 | Grinding chamber, sweeper blades, outlet chute | Clean: open the lid; brush and vacuum out leftover flour. Use no water on the stones | End of each use; daily in monsoon | Leftover flour picks up moisture and then cakes, moulds, goes rancid and draws insects. Caked flour also makes the sweepers drag and heats the next batch. |
| C03 | Grain intake | Inspect / test: check grain moisture is 14% w.b. or less (meter or salt-jar test) and look for stones and debris | Each use (each customer lot) | Damp grain glazes and loads the stone faces, raises flour temperature and puts moisture into the Sorel-bonded stones. Mineral debris wears the stones and the feed parts. |
| C04 | Gap setting / dial | Adjust: re-zero the dial at the touch point. Turn the runner by hand with power isolated and close the gap until it just drags. Never do this with the motor running | Weekly in season, and after any stone dressing | The stones wear, so the dial's zero drifts. If the dial is not re-zeroed, a "known" setting gives stone-on-stone contact (heat, grit in the flour) or coarse flour. |
| C05 | Hopper level sensor | Clean the capacitive sensor face. Test: let the grain fall below the sensor and check that the motor stops within about 60 s | Clean daily; test weekly | Flour and dust film, or damp residue, changes the capacitive reading. A sensor that reads "grain present" lets the stones run empty against each other. |
| C06 | Protections: E-stop, flour NTC, stone-speed trip | Test: the E-stop must latch and stop the motor. The flour-temperature reading must be plausible against ambient at start. The stone-rpm reading must be about 440 | Each use (E-stop); weekly (others) | These are the only automatic stops. An unchecked failed trip gives a false sense of cover. |
| C07 | Belt slip check | Inspect: compare the stone rpm on the display with nominal (440 rpm at 2,800 rpm motor) under load | Each use | The stone-speed trip acts only below 250 rpm, so slip of up to about 40% goes undetected. Slip glazes and heats the EPDM belt, and nothing senses belt temperature. Steady slip means the gravity tension has been lost (seized pivot, C18) or the belt is worn (C16). |
| C08 | PV array glass | Clean: soft brush or sponge with clean water, early morning or evening while the glass is cool. Start with the lower (drawbar) edge | Every 1 to 2 weeks in the dusty season; after dust storms | Soiling cuts charging. At the fixed 10° tilt, rain does not fully self-clean, and dirt banks up along the lower frame edge. Abrasive cleaning damages the anti-reflective coating, and cold water on hot glass (above 55 °C) risks thermal shock. |
| C09 | Battery cabinet louvre filter mats | Clean: wash and dry the polyester mats, or replace them | Every 2 weeks in the dry season; monthly otherwise; replace yearly | The cabinet has no fan, so cooling is by natural draft only. A clogged mat traps solar heat in the dark green cabinet. Cells age faster above about 45 °C, and only cell 8 is sensed. |
| C10 | Motor TEFC fan cowl and fins | Clean: blow or brush flour and chaff out of the cowl intake and the frame fins. Check the plastic fan for cracks | Weekly in season (daily in heavy dust) | Packed fins and a blocked cowl cut cooling. The winding NTC then derates the motor, so throughput drops long before the 130 °C trip. A cracked fan can also shed blades. |
| C11 | Lower spindle bearing (30208, grease nipple) | Lubricate with NSF H1 lithium-complex NLGI 2 grease only. Give about 3 g (2 to 3 gun strokes; replenishment rule 0.002 × D × B = 0.002 × 80 × 19.75 mm). Pump slowly, with the stone turned by hand. Never pump until grease appears | Every 250 h (about every 2 to 3 weeks in season), and before storage | Grease degrades with heat, and moisture and dust get in. A vertical shaft and dusty, humid service both cut the regrease interval roughly in half compared with the catalogue value. There is no relief port, so over-greasing blows out the lip seals or churns and overheats the bearing. Mixing grease types can soften the grease and destroy the food-grade status. |
| C12 | Lower bearing seals and preload | Inspect the double lip seals for grease weeping and dust ingress. Check axial play (lift-test the runner hub with a dial indicator, or feel). Re-set the locknut and tab washer if play is present | Monthly; after any rough tow | Tapered roller bearings lose preload as they run in and wear. Play then lets the runner wobble and the grinding gap become uneven. Towing with the 38 kg runner unsupported shock-loads the rollers (false brinelling and dents). |
| C13 | Upper spindle bearing (6208-2RS, aluminium housing) | Inspect: listen and feel for roughness or heat after a run (hand or IR thermometer). At the stones-off service, check the outer ring for creep (polished or fretted bore, red or black dust) and the housing bore for wear. Replace the bearing | Monthly check; replace at about 4,000 h (about 3 seasons), or sooner on roughness or seal damage | Sealed for life, so it cannot be regreased. It sits in the dust zone 150 mm below the hot stones. Aluminium expands about twice as much as steel, so a hot housing loosens on the steel outer ring (H7 fit). The ring can then creep and wear the soft bore. Contact seals wear in abrasive dust. |
| C14 | Feed agitator finger and UHMW-PE cam | Inspect the spring-steel finger for cracks, bending or a broken tip, and the cam lobes for wear. Replace both | Inspect daily; replace the finger about every 500 h (about each half-season); replace the cam on visible lobe wear | Six lobes at 440 rpm works the finger about 2,640 times a minute, roughly 158 million cycles per 1,000 h. That is fatigue territory. A broken finger tip falls downstream of the magnet, straight into the eye of the stones. Cam wear reduces shaking and starves the feed. |
| C15 | Feed spout pivot pin and UHMW-PE bushes | Inspect for play and for pin wear. Clean grain dust out of the pivot. Replace the bushes, and the pin if it is grooved | Monthly; replace the bushes each season | They run dry inside the grain stream, so mineral dust makes the contact abrasive. Play misdirects the feed away from the stone eye. |
| C16 | Poly-V belt (PJ10, EPDM) | Inspect the tension pointer position and the belt's ribs for rib-material loss, uneven wear, chunking or glazing. Replace when the pointer reaches "END" or on damage | Weekly; after every tow (check the belt is seated in the grooves) | EPDM poly-V belts wear by losing rib material, often without cracking, and lengthen as they do. That is why the pointer is the main wear gauge. With no tension lock, the motor plate bounces during towing and can unseat the belt or shock-load it. |
| C17 | Pulleys and fixings | Inspect the groove condition (wear, polish, rust in the grooves). Check that the motor pulley set screw over the key is tight. Check both taper-lock grub screws on the stone pulley, and the four bolt heads the speed sensor reads | Monthly; and at each belt change | A single set screw over a key loosens under cyclic load, after which the key and keyway fret. A loose taper-lock walks off the spindle. Worn grooves wear new belts out early. A missing bolt head gives false low stone rpm. |
| C18 | Motor plate pivot (20 mm pin, sintered bronze bushes) | Lubricate with a few drops of light machine oil (about SAE 20 to 30) on the bushes. Check the plate swings freely through its full range by lifting the motor | Monthly; after the monsoon, and after washing the trailer | Gravity tensioning works only while the pivot is free. Dust and rust can seize the pin, freezing tension at a lower value as the belt stretches, which causes slip (C07). The bushes' oil depletes over time, and grease clogs the pores of sintered bronze. |
| C19 | Motor phase / Hall cable at the swinging plate | Inspect the 300 mm free loop and the nylon gland on the motor for cracking, kinking, chafe and sheath splits. Tug-test the gland. Watch for intermittent stall or Hall faults | Monthly; after rough tows | The cable flexes every time the plate moves (belt stretch, load changes, towing bounce). Fatigue concentrates at the gland exit. The 0.5 mm² Hall and thermistor cores break first, causing intermittent stall trips or a lost motor temperature reading. |
| C20 | Motor bearings (6205-2RS) | Inspect: listen for noise and check for shaft play with the belt off. Replace | Check each belt change; replace at about 6,000 to 8,000 h, or on noise | The belt side-load and towing vibration wear the sealed bearings. Their grease life is finite and they cannot be regreased. |
| C21 | Stones: thickness and furrows | Inspect furrow depth (new: 3 mm) and stone thickness against the 30 mm groove. Re-dress the furrows when they are about 1 mm deep or the faces are glazed. Replace as a matched pair at the groove | Monthly measurement; dress as needed (roughly every 300 to 600 h, depending on grain) | Shallow furrows cannot move the meal outward, so throughput falls and flour temperature rises. Stones below 30 mm risk the bond cracking and expose the steel ring and backing. |
| C22 | Runner hub taper nut; stone rings and backing | Inspect: the hub nut must be tight. Check the cement backing and the stone-to-ring joint for cracks, softening or white efflorescence | Monthly; after the monsoon | The Sorel (magnesium oxychloride) bond softens and leaches with water. Signs show first at the edges and in the backing. A loose hub lets the runner wobble. |
| C23 | Gap mechanism: M24 trapezoidal screw, three guide posts, relief springs | Clean flour paste off the threads and posts. Lubricate them lightly with H1 food-grade grease. Test that the bed stone lifts freely by levering it about 3 mm and checking it springs back. Inspect the springs for rust and loss of free length | Monthly; after monsoon season | Flour plus humidity cakes the posts. A seized post defeats the 6 mm relief, so a hard object then hammers the stones and bearings. The zinc-plated springs rust in monsoon conditions, which changes their rate and can drop rust flakes near the product. |
| C24 | Sweeper blade rubber tips | Inspect for wear or tears. Replace | Monthly; replace when no longer touching the drum floor | Worn tips leave flour behind in the drum (heat, mould, cross-contamination between lots) and slow discharge. |
| C25 | Stone housing lid and latches; drum and chute | Inspect the over-centre latches, hinge and seal surfaces. Check for dents that rub the runner | Monthly | A latch opening in transit or in use exposes the rotating stone and lets in rain, which damages the Sorel bond. |
| C26 | Battery cell busbar joints (aluminium M6 studs, steel flange nuts, no lock washers) | Inspect with the isolator open and insulated tools. Torque-check to the cell maker's value (typically about 4 to 6 N·m for M6 aluminium studs; do not exceed it). Look for discoloration or corrosion. Under load, compare joint temperatures with an IR camera or thermometer | Monthly in towing season; after first 2 tows of a new pack; before each season | Aluminium studs creep and relax under clamp load and heat cycles. Towing vibration loosens flange nuts without lock washers. A loose joint heats up, and that heat goes unsensed except at cell 8. It can also look to the BMS like a "weak cell" (a voltage error under load) and trigger nuisance cutoffs. |
| C27 | Battery cells: balance and SOC calibration | Test: read the 16 cell voltages near full charge; the spread should be under about 30 to 50 mV. Fully charge until balancing runs (above 3.40 V per cell) and the SOC resets | Monthly in season; definitely after a cloudy spell | Passive balancing (60 mA) works only above 3.40 V per cell and is slow: 5 Ah of imbalance takes about 80 h in that window. In monsoon the pack may never reach full, so cells drift apart and the coulomb-counted SOC drifts until the display is wrong. |
| C28 | Battery cabinet: temperature check, sun exposure, door seal, structure | Inspect: on hot days, measure cabinet air and the outer-cell surfaces against cell 8. Park so the drawbar end is shaded. Check cell compression and fixing (cells must not move), the cabinet seal and louvres, and the paint | Monthly; on hot afternoons in season | Outer cells take the morning and evening sun through the dark cabinet wall. They can run hotter than cell 8, the one sensed cell, so the 55 °C charge cutoff may never fire while the outer cells age quickly. |
| C29 | 125 A Class-T fuse, 35 mm² lugs, isolator | Inspect the lug crimps and terminal torque, the fuse-holder contacts and the isolator for heat marks. Operate the isolator a few times | Every 3 months; before storage | Vibration and thermal cycling loosen the heavy terminals, and high-current joints overheat. The isolator is the storage disconnect, so it must work (C33). |
| C30 | Electrical enclosure interior | Inspect for water droplets, condensation tide marks, flour dust and corrosion on the terminals. Dry it out and remove dust (dry brush) | Weekly in monsoon; monthly otherwise | The enclosure has no drain. Daily temperature swings make it breathe humid air through the vent and gasket, and condensate collects with nowhere to go. Nothing senses the inside temperature or humidity. |
| C31 | Enclosure ePTFE vent (top face) and neoprene door gasket | Inspect: clean the vent cap by gentle brushing only (no solvent, no compressed air close up). Replace the vent if it is blocked or water-stained. Check the gasket for compression set or cracks. Check that the cable gland nut is tight | Monthly; replace the vent yearly | On the top face, the vent faces rain, mineral dust and flour dust from the mill next to it. A clogged membrane stops pressure equalising, so the enclosure pulls air in past the gasket and glands instead. That defeats IP65. |
| C32 | Controller terminals (rising-cage signal blocks with no ferrules; M6 brass power studs) | Inspect and re-tighten the signal screws. Look for splayed or broken strands. Fit ferrules when re-terminating. Torque-check the phase and battery studs. Watch for heat discoloration | Every 3 months; after the first month of use | Bare fine-stranded wire in screw terminals settles, and vibration loosens it. The result is intermittent Hall, speed or hopper signals, which show up as nuisance stall or speed trips, or as a lost thermistor reading. Loose high-current studs overheat. |
| C33 | Battery: storage preparation | Before storage: bring the pack to about 60 to 70% SOC. Grease C11, clean everything, then open the isolator. Every 4 weeks in storage, check pack voltage and SOC, and recharge to about 60% if needed | Before storage; monthly during lay-up | With the isolator closed the system draws about 45 mA, which over 12 weeks is about 91 Ah, roughly 86% of the 105 Ah pack. Charging can also stop while that drain continues (in monsoon cloud, or when the MPPT refuses to start because damp lowers PV insulation). The BMS cuts off at 2.60 V, but its own 12 mA draw keeps pulling the cells down toward damaging over-discharge. With the isolator open, the loss is about 24 Ah (about 23%) over 12 weeks. |
| C34 | Mill during storage | Turn the runner stone by hand several turns. Check for rodents, insects and moisture in the hopper and drum. Keep the lid closed and the canopy over the mill | Every 2 to 4 weeks of lay-up; before restart | This moves grease to new places on the rollers and seals and stops standstill corrosion marks. It also finds infestation, and damp that would soften the stone bond. |
| C35 | PV module clamps and rails (aluminium, A2 stainless, galvanized steel) | Inspect for white rust or zinc loss on the rails around the clamps, white aluminium-oxide pitting on the frames, and clamp bolt torque. Fit insulating pads or washers (EPDM or similar) between the dissimilar metals at the next service | Every 3 months, and after the monsoon; after rough tows | Aluminium, stainless steel and zinc in direct contact, wetted by monsoon rain, make galvanic cells that corrode the zinc and aluminium. Towing vibration also loosens the clamps, and a loose module can rattle, crack its glass or come away. |
| C36 | PV wiring: MC4 connectors, homerun cable, cable ties, gland | Inspect the connectors for heat or discoloration and full latch engagement. Check the homerun for chafe where it touches the galvanized leg edges. Replace UV-cracked nylon ties (or upgrade to coated stainless ties) | Every 3 months; replace the ties every 2 years or when brittle | UV and heat embrittle "UV-stabilized" nylon too, and towing vibration then rubs freed cables on steel edges. Chafe causes the insulation faults that stop charging (the 100 kΩ test). Loose MC4s arc and heat up. |
| C37 | PV insulation fault follow-up | Test: if charging fails to start in the morning (insulation fault), dry the connectors and gland. Megger each PV conductor to the frame at about 500 V DC with the array disconnected from the MPPT | When the fault code appears; yearly as a check | The MPPT tests only at wake-up and only refuses to charge. It does not locate the fault, and it does not catch faults that develop later in the day. |
| C38 | Canopy structure | Inspect the leg welds and bolts, and look for cracks at the base and the rail connections | Every 3 months; after rough roads | About 1.6 kW of panels on a fixed canopy, shaken by towing over unpaved roads, means cyclic bending loads. Fatigue cracks start at the leg welds. |
| C39 | Mill frame rubber mounts and deck bolts | Inspect the rubber for cracking, perishing or compression set. Torque-check the bolts | Every 3 months | The mounts isolate grinding vibration and take towing loads. Perished rubber passes shocks into the bearings and lets the frame shift, which knocks the belt line out. |
| C40 | Trailer coupling, safety chain, lights | Inspect the hitch latch and wear, safety chain and lights. Check the drawbar for cracks | Before each tow | Safety-critical. The battery cabinet at the drawbar end loads the coupling. |
| C41 | Tyres and wheel nuts | Inspect tyre pressure, tread, sidewall cracking and age. Torque-check the wheel nuts. Replace tyres at about 6 years of age even if the tread remains | Before each tow; re-torque nuts after the first 50 km following any wheel removal | 640 kg on 13-inch wheels on rough roads. UV ages the rubber of a part-time trailer before the tread wears out. |
| C42 | Wheel hub bearings | Inspect for play and noise and check hub temperature after a tow. Repack or regrease | Check monthly in towing season; repack yearly (before season) | Water gets into the hubs at river crossings and in monsoon rain, and long standing leaves condensation and fretting. |
| C43 | Leaf springs and U-bolts | Inspect for cracked leaves, shifted leaves and loose U-bolts. Re-torque the U-bolts | Every 3 months; before season | Unpaved roads fatigue the leaves. Loose U-bolts let the axle shift and the trailer pull to one side. |
| C44 | Galvanized and painted steel generally (trailer, frame, cabinet) | Inspect for white or red rust, chips and chafe. Touch up with zinc-rich paint or matching paint | After the monsoon; before storage | Monsoon humidity and road grit break down the coatings. The painted mild-steel mill frame and the C45 spindle corrode where they are bare. |
| C45 | Whole machine: post-tow check | Inspect: the belt is seated (C16), the runner turns freely by hand with no grinding noise, no fasteners are lost, no cables are chafed, and the cabinet cells have not shifted | After each tow, before grinding | The runner stone and the motor plate are unrestrained in transit, and towing is the machine's harshest load case. |

## 2. Do not

1. **Do not wash the stones, or let them stand wet.** The magnesium oxychloride (Sorel) bond is non-hydraulic and softens and leaches in liquid water. Clean them dry, and keep the lid closed in rain.
2. **Do not grind grain wetter than 14% w.b.** It glazes and clogs the stones, raises flour temperature toward the 55 °C trip, and puts moisture into the bond.
3. **Do not run the stones empty or close the gap under power.** Stone-on-stone contact wears the faces, overheats them and puts grit into the next flour. Find the touch point by hand with the isolator open.
4. **Do not over-grease the lower spindle bearing, or use non-H1 or incompatible grease.** There is no relief port, so excess grease blows out the lip seals or churns and overheats the bearing. Non-H1 grease can contaminate flour, and mixed thickeners can soften.
5. **Do not dress, oil or spray the belt, or fit a tensioning screw or prop under the motor plate during operation.** The belt is designed to run dry. Dressing or oil degrades EPDM and causes slip. A prop defeats gravity tensioning, so the belt slips as it stretches.
6. **Do not grease the sintered bronze motor-plate bushes.** Grease clogs the pores. Use a few drops of light oil.
7. **Do not over-torque the battery's aluminium M6 studs or use a power tool on them.** Aluminium threads strip and studs pull out. A damaged stud means replacing the cell.
8. **Do not leave the isolator closed during a lay-up without monthly checks.** The 45 mA draw can empty the pack in about 3 months. With charging blocked, the BMS's own 12 mA then over-discharges it.
9. **Do not disconnect MC4 connectors or open the PV string fuses under load (in daylight while charging).** At about 99 V open-circuit voltage, DC arcs do not self-extinguish. Stop charging first by opening the isolator.
10. **Do not clean the PV glass with abrasives or high pressure, or splash cold water on hot glass.** This strips the anti-reflective coating and risks thermal shock.
11. **Do not tow with grain in the hopper, the stone lid unlatched, or the gap closed to stone contact.** A loaded hopper adds load to the unsupported spindle. Vibration with the stones touching chips the faces.
12. **Do not exceed 40 km/h, or take rough roads fast.** The runner stone (38 kg) is unsupported on its bearings, and the motor plate is unrestrained. Shock loads brinell the bearings and unseat the belt.
13. **Do not pressure-wash the trailer near the electrical enclosure, vent, motor (IP54) or battery louvres.** IP65 and IP54 do not cover high-pressure jets, and water reaches the stones.
14. **Do not mix new and used cells, or replace a single cell without matching its voltage and capacity first.** A mismatched cell sets the whole pack's usable capacity and stresses the BMS's weak passive balancing.

## 3. Not covered by the machine's own sensors and protections (manual checks needed)

- **Spindle bearings (upper 6208, lower 30208):** no temperature or vibration sensing. Check by hand or IR thermometer and by listening, and check play (C11 to C13).
- **Belt:** no temperature or slip sensing. The stone-speed trip acts only below 250 rpm, so partial slip, glazing and wear show only on the pointer and in the rpm comparison (C07, C16).
- **Battery temperature, other than cell 8:** outer cells heated through the dark cabinet wall, cabinet air and hot busbar joints are all unsensed (C26, C28).
- **Busbar and terminal joint resistance:** not measured. It may show up only as cell-voltage error under load and nuisance cutoffs (C26, C29, C32).
- **Cell imbalance and SOC drift:** balancing works only above 3.40 V, and the SOC display can be wrong after long partial charging (C27).
- **Standby discharge during storage:** nothing warns that the 45 mA or 12 mA drains are emptying the pack (C33).
- **Electrical enclosure interior:** no temperature or humidity sensing, and no drain, so condensation and vent blockage must be checked by eye (C30, C31).
- **PV insulation:** tested only at morning wake-up, and only blocks charging. Daytime faults, and the battery or DC side of the system, are not tested (C36, C37).
- **Galvanic corrosion and clamp loosening at the PV mounts** (C35).
- **Mechanical wear:** stone thickness and furrow depth, feed finger fatigue or fracture (downstream of the magnet), cam and bush wear, guide-post seizure that disables the 6 mm relief, sweeper tips, pulley fixings and motor-cable flex damage. The only electrical sign of cable damage is an intermittent stall trip after damage has already occurred (C14 to C24).
- **Magnet loading:** nothing indicates when the magnet is saturated with tramp iron (C01).
- **Trailer running gear:** tyres, hubs, springs, coupling (C40 to C43).
