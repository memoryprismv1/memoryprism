## 0. Opening: a rejected starting question

The assistant first proposed: *why do some old bridges and buildings outlast much newer ones?*

The person rejected the premise: "I don't know if it's true. Do they actually outlast or they have become landmarked and require a lot more additional resources to keep around."

Research confirmed the objection splits the question three ways: survivorship (cheap old buildings were demolished or collapsed, so we see the best of the bunch), upkeep (heritage status buys maintenance), and genuine material or design durability (e.g. self-healing lime clasts in Roman concrete). Only the third is about building quality.

**Lesson for the method:** the assistant must not offer a starting premise it has not checked. Questioning the premise is the method working. Starting questions should be ones the person can verify themselves.

## 1. Cut apple browning

Starting question: *Why does a cut apple turn brown, and why does lemon juice slow it?* Chosen because the person can check the premise in 20 minutes.

**Person's first move:** "Looks like surface area + chemistry (lemon juice acid, browning oxidation, etc)."

**Research:** close, but the trigger is broken cells, not surface area as such. Polyphenol oxidase (PPO) and its polyphenol substrates sit in separate compartments (plastids and vacuoles). Cutting tears the walls; enzyme, substrate and oxygen meet; o-quinones form and polymerise into brown melanin. Lemon juice works mainly through acid pushing pH below PPO's working range (citric acid is ~95% of lemon juice acids); vitamin C plays a minor role. Sugar or syrup coatings work by limiting oxygen.

**Person's inventory:** "Board, flow, accumulation, constraints (peel), editors, maybe ledger in terms of exposure."

**Assistant's response to the map (concept by concept):**
- Board: nested (cell / slice / tree and orchard); the answer changes with scale.
- Constraints: peel confirmed (passive oxygen and damage barrier); added pH range, variety, temperature.
- Flow: oxygen diffusion; enzyme–substrate mixing after walls tear.
- Accumulation: melanin builds up and stays; also P-Memory (a bruise records where an apple was dropped; the person reading it is the translator).
- Editors: none on the slice (chemistry only); editors appear at orchard scale (pathogens, insects, human breeders selecting low-browning varieties).
- Ledger: "exposure" fits Flow better; Ledger asks what the apple pays to stay intact. A living cell spends energy keeping compartments sealed; browning is what happens when payments stop. Labelled inference.

**Emerging model:** browning as a loaded trap: parts kept apart while the cell lives and pays; they mix only where cells break.

## 2. Internal browning with no external sign

The person asked whether the assistant wanted to check if internal browning can happen without external signs. Research found it is a named class of storage disorders: browning in the mid-cortex "without any external sign", not detectable externally except in severe cases; physiological (not pathogenic). Mechanism for CO₂ injury: oxidative stress → membrane damage → enzymatic oxidation of phenolics. A Honeycrisp study linked injury to shortages in energy and reducing agents, which supports the Ledger reading directly. Twist: causes include cold and excess CO₂, not only ageing, so the better rule is "browning wherever cells can't keep their compartments sealed". Detail: in severe CO₂ cases a healthy white rim remains just below the skin, where gas escapes most easily.

## 3. Transfer to bananas

Unprompted, the person connected this to earlier curiosity about bananas over-ripening. The apple model carried over in four of five respects:
1. Sugar spots are melanin from polyphenols such as L-DOPA (same chemistry family).
2. Oxygen is still required: film packaging prevented early spotting mainly through low oxygen; high oxygen promoted spotting.
3. Fridge blackening is chilling injury: below ~12 °C peel cells die and darken; pulp unaffected.
4. The browning chemistry is concentrated in the outer peel (phenolics and the only rising PPO activity), so for bananas "peel as constraint" needs a tweak: the peel is where the trap sits.
5. Difference: in one study banana spotting did not correlate with measured PPO activity; dopamine depletion did. Same trap, different limiting ingredient.

## 4. Geometry of over-ripening

The person clarified their curiosity: what patterns does over-ripening follow: geometry, linear, radial? Research produced six shapes and a working rule (assistant-assembled, labelled as a working rule, not a law):

| Shape | Example | Likely control |
|---|---|---|
| Spots that start at points then stall | Banana sugar spots from stomata; stall attributed to oxygen collapse in a reaction–diffusion model | Gas through pores |
| Centre damaged, rim spared | Apple CO₂ injury | Gas through the skin |
| Spokes | Radial watercore: ~10 lenticular areas around the 10 vascular bundles | Plumbing |
| Inside-out | Pears soften from the core; apple core flush spreads in wedges | Which tissue ages first |
| End to end | Tomato colour "breaks" at the blossom end | Which tissue ages first |
| Bands | Cold-storage browning in the outer cortex | Layer-specific conditions |

The pattern is P-Memory; the observer is the translator reading mechanism from shape.

## 5. Fruit drop: the person's thought experiments

**Person:** "If a fruit stays too long on the tree, it has to fall off and then release seeds. So likely breaking attachment will be primary part. Why? You don't want the rot to travel up."

**Check:**
- Exists: abscission is an active programme (resorption → protective layer → detachment). The tree builds a suberin/lignin cork seal on its side.
- Exists: hormone handshake. Auxin flow from the fruit keeps the abscission zone insensitive; auxin depletion makes it sensitive to ethylene, the ripening gas.
- Not supported as primary: stated functions are seed dispersal and carbon balance; rot control as *the* reason is the person's hypothesis.
- Counter-case: brown-rot mummies typically stay attached, and infection can spread into twigs (cankers). Blossoms killed by brown rot stay attached; frost-killed ones fall. Inference: letting go needs living tissue, so a fungus that takes over first may block the seal.

**Person:** "If a tree is keeping the rot, it will drop the healthy… It won't let unhealthy and healthy continuity devices share a lot of space unless the seeds are super shielded, then the rot is seed germination food (after composting)."

**Check (assistant first fact-checked like a reviewer, then was corrected by the person; see below):**
- Trees do shed damaged fruit early (codling-moth-infested apples, sawfly fruitlets in June drop): partial support for separating healthy and unhealthy.
- Rot as seed food: evidence mostly against for fleshy fruit. A meta-analysis (preprint) found gut passage raises germination mainly through pulp removal, and pulp removal lowers fungal mortality. Janzen's hypothesis that microbes spoil fruit to deter animals is noted as debated.
- Shielded-seed case (stone fruit pit) left as an open question.
- Three editors on one board: tree (shedding), fungus (holding and spreading), animals (preferring clean fruit).

## 6. The correction that defined the method

The person: "This is a thinking exercise. A thought experiment… If this happens what background processes are required."

The assistant then reframed the "keeps the rot, drops the healthy" idea as required processes:

| Required process | Status |
|---|---|
| Detect which fruit is sick | Partly exists (damaged fruit ripen early and drop) |
| Decide which to keep | No evidence of a tree comparing fruits; each fruit appears to run its own programme |
| Signal the attachment to let go or hold | Exists (auxin/ethylene) |
| Seal each break | Exists (cork layer) |
| Pay for it | Implied: sensing, signalling and sealing cost energy |

What the person's logic reached before research confirmed it: deliberate detachment, a protective seal on the tree side, early shedding of damaged fruit, and a decision signal between fruit and stalk.

## Findings for Memory Prism

1. In the hands of a non-specialist, MP's questions produced correct structural hypotheses, which research then confirmed or refined.
2. Transfer worked: the apple model carried to bananas with one instructive difference.
3. The assistant's useful role was: answer the settled part briefly, respond to the person's map, convert thought experiments into required processes, check each, and treat mismatches as another player (here, the fungus).
4. Failure modes of the assistant observed: unchecked starting premise; grading thought experiments as claims; leaving homework for the person.
5. These lessons were drafted into `skill/memory-prism/references/person-led.md`.
