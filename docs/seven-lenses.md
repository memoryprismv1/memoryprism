# The Seven Lenses

The seven essays are a library of optional lenses. They are not a seven-step sequence. Select only the lens or lenses that help open the specific topic.

## Boards
**Entry question:** Which constraints and configurations make this possible? What changes when the board changes?

Pay attention to boundaries, scale, budgets, transport, disturbance, turnover, and nested conditions when relevant.

## Audits
**Entry question:** Is anything detecting deviation and correcting it, or does the system simply cross a threshold?

Do not assume that detection or correction exists in a non-living or otherwise non-self-maintaining system.

## Identity
**Entry question:** What makes this count as the same system? How is membership maintained under limited resources?

Use this when continuity, boundary, or component membership is part of the problem—not as a mandatory question for every pile, process, or event.

## Flow Under Constraint
**Entry question:** What moves along which gradients or routes? How do repeated flows become paths, chokepoints, or dependencies?

Possibility alone does not ensure that a route is viable; cost and constraints shape which flows persist.

## Working Lines
**Entry question:** Which pathways enable function, and how might those same pathways transmit stress or failure?

The useful pattern is structural: pathways that carry normal operation can also concentrate stress or transmit disruption. Establish the mechanism in each domain rather than assuming equivalence.

## No Sacred States
**Entry question:** Are we treating a behaviour or state as intrinsic when it depends on surrounding conditions?

A material or system does not get to ignore the constraints of the conditions it occupies. Keep the specific physical or domain mechanism explicit.

## Accumulation Patterns
**Entry question:** What turns contact or repeated presence into connected structure? Which parts carry load, provide filler or reserve, and why might the structure be cheaper to maintain than dismantle?

Proximity is not automatically cohesion; cohesion is not automatically connectivity; connectivity does not mean every part carries the same load.

## The lens test

After applying a lens, ask:

> Did this lens change what I noticed, inferred, researched, or asked next?

If not, drop it from the output. The goal is a better next question, not proof that the full vocabulary can be applied everywhere.
