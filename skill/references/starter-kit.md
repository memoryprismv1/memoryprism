# Memory Prism Starter Kit — Checklist v1.0

This is the canonical checklist reference for the platform-neutral skill.

## Purpose

Help a person enter an unfamiliar topic and find a productive first route of inquiry.

This is a starter kit, not an exhaustive audit, a substitute for domain knowledge, a complete answer, or a wiki generator. It should accelerate inquiry without prescribing the route or taking curiosity away from the person.

The checklist has done its job when it helps identify a useful next move. Completion is not the goal.

## Core concepts in the original Framework

The v1.0 Framework PDF presents these as its main concepts and sections:

- **Memory**
- **Constraints**
- **Editors**
- **Board**
- **Legacy**
- **Ledger / energy accounting**
- **Living matter**
- **Architecture**
- **Stability**

Memory, Constraints, Editors and Board provide the primary structural orientation. The original PDF also gives Ledger a dedicated major section (“Show me the money, honey”), explaining energy in, energy out, and the minimum payments required to keep structure intact. It returns to open and closed ledgers when explaining living matter. **Ledger is part of the original Framework core; it is not an addition to this checklist or one of the optional essay lenses.**

Use **P-Memory (Persistent Memory)** as the retrieval label for the Framework's Memory concept. The label does not create a new concept or change the Framework's definition.

The seven supporting essays provide optional lenses for deeper examination. They do not replace the Framework's core concepts. The Foundation essay **Boards** deepens the core Board concept; it does not make Board optional.

## The starter checklist

Use these as a first pass. Do not answer every question automatically. Skip anything that adds no explanatory value.

### 1. What am I actually trying to understand?

When identifying the Memory concept in an inventory, use **P-Memory (Persistent Memory)** as the retrieval label. It refers to persistent structure shaped by past interactions, including naturally occurring physical structures and phenomena. An intended goal or deliverable is one case, not a prerequisite; intention, storage, and recognition are not required. The canonical Framework remains authoritative.

- What is the observed thing, event, pattern, or puzzle?
- What needs explaining: its existence, behaviour, persistence, spread, change, or failure?
- What unit and timescale make the question tractable?

### 2. What is the board?

- What is the survival environment at the relevant scale and timescale?
- Which **constraints** passively bound the possible configurations?
- Which **editors** actively filter which viable configurations persist, change or disappear?
- Are there nested boards, local micro-conditions, or higher-level boards that alter what is possible?

A Board is not just a backdrop. It is a time- and scale-specific survival environment composed of constraints and editors. Keep the distinctions clear: constraints bound possibilities; editors differentially shape persistence within those bounds. Neither requires human intent.

### 3. What does the Ledger show?

Ledger is part of the original Framework. It accounts for energy in, energy out, and the minimum payments required to keep structure intact.

- What energy and other resources are available now?
- What is stored as a reserve, and what must be acquired continuously?
- What is consumed by operation, maintenance, repair, growth or recovery?
- What are the minimum payments required to keep the structure intact?
- How much reserve or operating margin remains, and over what time?
- What happens when inflow or reserves fall below maintenance requirements?
- What can be reduced, shed, sacrificed or allowed to fail first?
- After a disturbance or failed change, is there enough resource and time left inside the correction window to recover?

The original Framework applies energy accounting to persistent systems generally, not only to living organisms. For living matter, dormant seeds and other reduced states retain open ledgers: payment is paused or deferred, not settled. For a seed, examine the stored energy reserve, the cost of maintaining viability, damage and repair during dormancy, and the resources needed for germination and early growth. For software, examine concrete capacities and budgets such as compute, storage, I/O, connections, locks, dry runs, timeouts, retries, operator time and rollback capacity. Do not turn every cost into a money metaphor.

### 4. What moves and accumulates?

**Flow**
- What is transferred or changed: matter, energy, information, people, pressure, money, effort or something else?
- What gradients, costs, paths, bottlenecks or barriers shape that movement?
- Which flows are inputs or expenditures that belong in the Ledger, rather than merely movements to describe?

**Accumulation**
- What gets concentrated, repeated, stored, dissipated, removed or left behind?
- Does repeated contact become cohesive, connected or load-bearing structure, or is it only a pile?
- Does any reserve, residue or filler affect how the arrangement works?

### 5. What makes the arrangement persist?

Keep these three questions available. Use only the ones that fit.

**4a. Pile check**
- Is this a connected structure, or merely things near one another?
- What distinguishes a pile, a cluster, and a structure that holds together?

**4b. Drift and maintenance**
- What happens if the arrangement is left alone?
- How quickly does it drift, degrade, disperse, or move away from its current state?
- What recurring work, energy, or input restores or maintains it?
- What is cheaper to include, tolerate, repair, replace, or remove?

**4c. Identity**
- What counts as the same entity, process, or group over time?
- If membership matters, how is it maintained or enforced under finite resources?

Use identity language only when continuity of the entity itself is part of the puzzle. Do not force it onto a pile or process that does not need it.

### 6. Where does it change, get interpreted, corrected, or fail?

- What thresholds or conditions change its state?
- Does any part of the system detect or interpret signals? If so, what reads them, what can it detect, and what happens if the signal is missed or misread?
- If the system actively maintains itself, what detects deviation and what corrects it? What is delayed, unaffordable, or beyond correction?
- Which paths enable normal function, and could those same paths transmit stress or failure?
- What did earlier events leave behind that changes the present?
- Is this a change within broadly unchanged constraints, or did a higher-level board change the rules?

Do not assume every system detects, interprets, corrects, anticipates, or governs anything. Some systems simply undergo physical transitions. A “translator” is relevant only where something actually converts a signal or state into an interpreted meaning or response.

### 7. What is the best next question?

- What remains uncertain?
- What are two plausible explanations that lead to different expectations?
- What observation, source, comparison, or small test could distinguish them?
- What would we expect to find under each explanation?

Prefer a next question that can change what we believe or do. If a settled answer exists, give it first; use the checklist only for the genuinely open part.

Stop when you have a useful direction.

## Seven optional lenses

These seven essay lenses are available for deeper examination; they are not substitutes for the original Framework concepts. Use none, one or several, depending on what changes the inquiry. There is no fixed maximum. Do not apply all seven by default, but do not suppress a useful lens to meet an arbitrary limit. A concrete candidate may be retained when it is likely to help a deeper follow-up.

- **Boards:** Deepen the core Board reading by examining nested boards, local and higher-level conditions, scale, dominance, thresholds and how a change at one level alters another. This lens does not make the core Board operator optional.
- **Audits:** Is there a system that maps its state against viability bounds and detects deviation? Does it actually correct, tolerate or fail to correct the deviation? Do not label every threshold or external measurement an audit.
- **Identity:** What counts as belonging to the system? How are components or members maintained, changed, repaired, tolerated or removed under finite resources?
- **Flow Under Constraint:** What moves along which gradients or routes? How do cost, transport capacity, convergence and bottlenecks shape whether a flow can continue?
- **Working Lines:** Which pathways carry force, energy or coordination, and how might those same pathways transmit stress or failure?
- **No Sacred States:** Are we assuming a material, component or process behaves identically when its board or conditions change?
- **Accumulation Patterns:** What turns contact or repeated presence into cohesive, connected or load-bearing structure? Which parts carry load, which provide filler or reserve, and why might a structure be cheaper to maintain than dismantle?

After using a lens, ask: **Did it earn its keep?** If not, leave it out. Similar-looking patterns are leads for investigation, not proof of the same mechanism.

## Assistant reasoning audit (AI-only)

For substantive exploratory work, the assistant should use these questions internally to examine how it constructed the investigation and answer. This is not an additional Memory Prism lens and is not a questionnaire for the user.

- What assumptions did it inherit?
- What information did it use or fail to use?
- What constraints shaped the answer?
- Which editor favoured that particular response?
- Where did the reasoning take a shortcut?
- What alternative structures could have produced a better result?

Use the audit to revise the reasoning rather than reciting it. Do not invent an editor where none is established. Surface only material findings.
