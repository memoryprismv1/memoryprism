# Memory Prism Starter Kit — Checklist v1.0

This is the canonical checklist reference for the platform-neutral skill.

## Purpose

Help a person enter an unfamiliar topic and find a productive first route of inquiry.

This is a starter kit, not an exhaustive audit, a substitute for domain knowledge, a complete answer, or a wiki generator. It should accelerate inquiry without prescribing the route or taking curiosity away from the person.

The checklist has done its job when it helps identify a useful next move. Completion is not the goal.

## The starter checklist

Use these as a first pass. Do not answer every question automatically. Skip anything that adds no explanatory value.

### 1. What am I actually trying to understand?

- What is the observed thing, event, pattern, or puzzle?
- What needs explaining: its existence, behaviour, persistence, spread, change, or failure?
- What unit and timescale make the question tractable?

### 2. What is the board?

- What conditions and constraints shape what can happen here?
- What sets the limits: space, time, physical laws, resources, budgets, learned conventions, institutional rules, or something else?
- What processes or pressures filter outcomes within those limits? For example, gravity constrains what can remain supported; selection, competition, enforcement, or resource scarcity can make some configurations less likely to persist.
- Are there nested boards, local micro-conditions, or higher-level conditions that change what is possible?

A board is the configuration of conditions under which the phenomenon occurs. Distinguish the conditions that set possibilities from the processes that filter outcomes; do not treat the distinction as a required vocabulary exercise.

Examples: a corner of a room can differ from the room as a whole; a learned convention can shape traffic or interface behaviour; a device at 40% charge can be in a different state from one at 100% without necessarily being on a different board.

### 3. What moves, accumulates, and costs something?

**Flow and accumulation**
- What is transferred or changed: matter, energy, information, people, pressure, money, effort, or something else?
- What gradients, costs, paths, bottlenecks, or barriers shape that movement?
- What gets concentrated, repeated, stored, dissipated, removed, or left behind?
- Does any reserve, residue, or filler affect how the arrangement works?

**The survival ledger**
- What ongoing cost or overhead is required to keep the arrangement going?
- What happens when inflow falls below maintenance needs?
- What gets reduced, shed, sacrificed, or allowed to fail first when the available budget no longer covers everything?

Use the ledger when costs and competing demands matter. Do not force a budget metaphor onto a topic where it adds nothing.

### 4. What makes the arrangement persist?

Keep these three questions available. Use only the ones that fit.

**4a. Pile check**
- Is this a connected structure, or merely things near one another?
- What distinguishes a pile, a cluster, and a structure that holds together?

**4b. Drift and maintenance**
- What happens if the arrangement is left alone?
- How quickly does it drift, degrade, disperse, or move away from its current state?
- What recurring work, energy, or input restores or maintains it?
- What is cheaper to include, tolerate, repair, replace, or remove?

**4c. Identity**
- What counts as the same entity, process, or group over time?
- If membership matters, how is it maintained or enforced under finite resources?

Use identity language only when continuity of the entity itself is part of the puzzle. Do not force it onto a pile or process that does not need it.

### 5. Where does it change, get interpreted, corrected, or fail?

- What thresholds or conditions change its state?
- Does any part of the system detect or interpret signals? If so, what reads them, what can it detect, and what happens if the signal is missed or misread?
- If the system actively maintains itself, what detects deviation and what corrects it? What is delayed, unaffordable, or beyond correction?
- Which paths enable normal function, and could those same paths transmit stress or failure?
- What did earlier events leave behind that changes the present?
- Is this a change within broadly unchanged constraints, or did a higher-level board change the rules?

Do not assume every system detects, interprets, corrects, anticipates, or governs anything. Some systems simply undergo physical transitions. A “translator” is relevant only where something actually converts a signal or state into an interpreted meaning or response.

### 6. What is the best next question?

- What remains uncertain?
- What are two plausible explanations that lead to different expectations?
- What observation, source, comparison, or small test could distinguish them?
- What would we expect to find under each explanation?

Prefer a next question that can change what we believe or do. If a settled answer exists, give it first; use the checklist only for the genuinely open part.

Stop when you have a useful direction.

## Seven optional lenses

These are a library of ways into a problem, not seven stages. Use one or two by default. Use another only when it changes what you notice, infer, research, or ask next.

- **Boards:** Which constraints and configurations make this possible? What changes when the board changes?
- **Audits:** Is anything detecting deviation and correcting it, or does the system simply cross a threshold?
- **Identity:** What makes this count as the same system? How is membership maintained under limited resources?
- **Flow Under Constraint:** What moves along which gradients or routes? How do repeated flows become paths, chokepoints, or dependencies?
- **Working Lines:** Which pathways enable function, and how might those same pathways transmit stress or failure?
- **No Sacred States:** Are we treating a behaviour or state as intrinsic when it depends on surrounding conditions?
- **Accumulation Patterns:** What turns contact or repeated presence into connected structure? Which parts carry load, which provide filler or reserve, and why might the structure be cheaper to maintain than dismantle?

After using a lens, ask: **Did it earn its keep?** If not, leave it out. Similar-looking patterns are leads for investigation, not proof of the same mechanism.
