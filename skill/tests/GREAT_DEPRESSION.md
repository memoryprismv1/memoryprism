# Test F — The Great Depression

**Purpose:** Test whether Memory Prism helps an assistant find a useful causal entry point into a large historical/economic event without forcing a full seven-lens analysis or substituting framework vocabulary for economic history.

## Prompt

> Run the Great Depression through Memory Prism. Explain how the downturn became prolonged and global, what mechanisms kept it going, and what changed the path toward recovery. Use evidence, distinguish facts from interpretation, and identify one useful unresolved question. Do not force every lens into the answer.

## Scope assumption

Treat the United States as the initial focal case, while explaining international transmission. The phrase “Great Depression” can refer to different national experiences; do not imply every country followed the same timeline or policy path.

## Evidence anchors

Use authoritative domain sources and cite them in the answer. These are starting points, not an exhaustive or definitive account:

- Federal Reserve History, [The Great Depression (1929–1941)](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/great-depression): banking panics, monetary contraction and deflation, Federal Reserve policy, reforms, and the 1937–38 setback.
- Federal Reserve History, [Banking Panics of 1930–31](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/banking-panics-1930-31): bank failures, cash hoarding, declining money supply, deflation, and international feedback through the gold standard.
- Federal Reserve History, [Banking Panics of 1931–33](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/banking-panics-1931-33): external gold drain, domestic bank runs, policy conflict, and the 1933 banking holiday.
- U.S. Department of State, [The Great Depression and U.S. Foreign Relations](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1921-1936/great-depression): global financial links, gold-standard rigidity, and weak international coordination.

## What a strong application should notice

1. **Start with the historical puzzle.** The 1929 crash matters, but it is not a sufficient single-cause explanation for a depression that deepened through successive banking and international crises.
2. **Choose lenses selectively.** Boards and Flow Under Constraint are plausible first choices: monetary and banking institutions, debt, the gold standard, and policy commitments constrained available responses; flows of deposits, credit, cash, gold, and trade transmitted shocks. Working Lines may be added only if it sharpens a specific, evidenced propagation mechanism.
3. **Name mechanisms, not just structural similarities.** Bank runs and failures reduced intermediation and the money supply; deflation increased real debt burdens and weakened spending and balance sheets; gold-standard commitments and policy choices transmitted or prolonged contraction. Keep the causal chain appropriately qualified.
4. **Keep multiple causal levels visible.** The stock-market crash, pre-existing vulnerabilities, banking panics, monetary policy, the international gold standard, and policy coordination are not interchangeable explanations. Do not collapse the history into “the crash caused everything” or “the Fed caused everything.”
5. **Treat recovery as a change in conditions and policy, not a single magic switch.** The 1933 banking holiday and banking reforms, suspension of gold convertibility and reflation, fiscal and relief programmes, and later wartime mobilisation had different roles and timings. Avoid claiming that one programme alone ended the Depression.
6. **Distinguish fact from inference.** Cite historical claims; mark contested causal weight as debated. A structural fit within Memory Prism is not independent evidence.
7. **Stop with traction.** End with one material uncertainty and a concrete comparison or research direction that could resolve it. Do not furnish a complete economic history unless asked.

## Lens-selection check

- **Boards:** Did institutional rules and international monetary arrangements change which responses were feasible?
- **Flow Under Constraint:** Did tracing money, credit, gold, and trade reveal a specific transmission or feedback mechanism?
- **Working Lines (optional):** Did an evidenced connection carry ordinary financial activity and also transmit stress?
- **Audits (usually omit):** Do not use “audit” as a synonym for central-bank policy or assume a coherent controller existed. If discussing detection, decision, and correction, specify who had which information and authority.
- **Identity, No Sacred States, Accumulation Patterns:** Include only if a concrete question makes one of them useful. Their presence in the framework does not make them mandatory here.

## Suggested discriminating question

Which mechanisms best explain why the initial downturn became a prolonged contraction: the propagation of banking panics and monetary deflation, the constraints imposed by the international gold standard, or their interaction with policy decisions?

A good next step compares the timing of bank failures, money/price contraction, and countries’ departure from gold with recovery timing. Do not assume the answer in advance; country-level differences and the 1937–38 U.S. recession are important checks against an overly simple account.

## Scoring rubric (0–2 each; maximum 12)

1. Directly answers why the downturn deepened, persisted, and spread.
2. Uses only one or two useful MP lenses by default and explains their value through the actual history.
3. Uses accurate, sourced economic and historical mechanisms.
4. Distinguishes initial shock, propagation mechanisms, institutional constraints, and policy response.
5. Separates evidence from inference and preserves genuine causal uncertainty.
6. Offers a concrete next comparison or research question without turning the response into a forced, exhaustive checklist.

**Score anchors:** 0 = absent or mishandled; 1 = partly useful or materially incomplete; 2 = clear, proportionate, and well supported.

## Failure signatures

- Treats the seven lenses as seven required stages.
- Uses “board,” “flow,” or “editor” as labels without identifying historical mechanisms.
- Treats analogy or framework coherence as proof.
- Gives a single-cause explanation or presents contested interpretations as settled.
- Says the New Deal alone ended the Depression, or that World War II alone explains every aspect of recovery, without qualification.
- Repeats a generic systems summary instead of researching or specifying the next discriminating comparison.
- Hands the research back to the user.
