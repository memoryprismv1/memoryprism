# Tin Whisker Growth — 2026-11-17

## Test type
Normal daily test. Topic selected independently of recent conversation topics and projects.

## Question
Why can a solid tin coating spontaneously grow long, hair-like crystalline protrusions, and how certain is the explanation?

## Observation
Tin whiskers are electrically conductive crystalline filaments that sometimes grow outward from tin-plated surfaces, particularly some electroplated finishes. They can reach several millimetres and, rarely, exceed 10 mm. If one bridges closely spaced conductors at different electrical potentials, it can cause a short circuit.

Whiskers are distinct from electrochemical dendrites. Dendrites typically grow along a surface through dissolved metal ions and electrochemical migration. Tin whiskers grow outward from the coating and do not require metal dissolution or an applied electric field.

## Mechanism and evidence
A leading family of explanations involves stress in the tin coating and transport of tin through its microstructure.

Potential sources of stress include:
- Residual stress introduced by electroplating chemistry and process conditions.
- Intermetallic formation at the coating/substrate interface, such as Cu6Sn5 in tin-on-copper systems.
- External compression, bending or stretching after plating.
- Scratches, handling damage and thermal-expansion mismatch.

NIST identifies compressive stress and impeded stress-relief mechanisms, such as restricted grain growth or recrystallisation, as important conditions. Grain structure influences how stress can relax. Its work found that changing the grain structure through pulsed deposition and bismuth enrichment could promote more uniform creep and reduce whisker/hillock formation.

An important qualification: copper–tin intermetallic growth is not the sole possible cause. NIST observed whiskers and hillocks on tungsten substrates, which do not form the same intermetallic compound with tin. Intrinsic deposition stress can therefore contribute independently.

NASA states that no single accepted explanation of the complete whisker-growth mechanism has been established. Stress relief, recrystallisation, abnormal grain growth and material transport remain active explanatory elements rather than a universally settled single mechanism.

## Why it matters
Whiskers can cause:
- Stable or transient electrical shorts.
- Conductive debris if they break loose.
- In certain electrical environments, destructive metal-vapour arcs.

The risk is difficult to exclude by initial inspection because incubation periods and growth rates vary widely. A component that appears acceptable at manufacture may develop a whisker later.

## Critical Thinking examination
- Do not mistake a proposed mechanism for a fully established one.
- Do not treat copper–tin intermetallic growth as the only possible cause.
- Do not confuse whiskers with dendrites.
- Do not infer long-term safety from a clean initial inspection.
- Do not assume a mitigation works under every material and environmental condition.

## Findings
A metal coating can retain a history of how it was deposited and mechanically treated. That history influences how it changes later, sometimes in ways that are difficult to predict. The phenomenon is a useful example of latent material state becoming visible only after time-dependent evolution.

## Limits and unresolved questions
- The detailed nucleation and growth mechanism is not fully settled.
- Growth is highly variable across plating chemistry, grain structure, substrate, thickness and environment.
- No simple initial inspection can guarantee absence of future whisker growth.
- Mitigation effectiveness must be evaluated for the actual component and operating conditions.
