## Question and conventional explanation
How do termite mounds exchange gases and regulate the nest environment?

Mounds are porous, connected structures with channels, chambers and walls whose geometry and material properties interact with weather and colony activity. A particularly well-measured mechanism occurs in *Odontotermes obesus*. A 2015 in-situ study measured airflow through surface conduits and found that daily temperature changes drive ventilation. Thin outer flutes heat and cool faster than deeper, larger conduits. During the day, warmer air in the flutes rises and cooler air descends through deeper chimneys, producing a convection loop; the flow reverses at night as the temperature pattern reverses. Geometry, heterogeneous thermal mass and porosity together create oscillating circulation.

This is not a universal explanation for every termite mound. Wind pressure, internal metabolic heating, mound orientation, wall permeability, tunnel geometry and species-specific construction can also affect airflow. A 2025 review describes porous networks, micropores and environmental adaptation, while emphasising unresolved questions. The mound alters exchange of heat, moisture and gases; colony activity, brood, fungus gardens, nest depth and behaviour also contribute to microclimate. It does not guarantee a fixed temperature or humidity.

## Critical Thinking examination
The tempting simplification is “termites build chimneys that work like air conditioners.” Evidence is narrower:
- The 2015 study measured flow in *O. obesus* and linked daily reversal to temperature differences across mound conduits.
- Mound architecture is part of the mechanism, not just a passive shell around a conventional chimney.
- Wind and metabolic heat may contribute in other contexts, but should not replace the measured mechanism in that study.
- Gas exchange, temperature regulation and humidity regulation are related but distinct outcomes.
- Similar-looking mounds may have different internal porosity and connectivity.
- The 2025 review identifies open questions; there is no single definitive ventilation theory for all termites.
