## Question
How do individual fireflies produce coordinated flashing patterns across a group?

## Conventional explanation
There is no single universal firefly synchronisation mechanism. Flashing patterns and social behaviour vary across species. In some Southeast Asian fireflies, males gather in trees or other display sites and produce coordinated flashes; visual signals and interactions between individuals are important to synchrony. Species-specific flash timing is also part of courtship communication.

A particularly informative case is the synchronous firefly *Photinus carolinus* in the Great Smoky Mountains. Its males produce rapid synchronous flashes in bursts, with collective bursts recurring about every 12 seconds. A key experimental finding complicates the simple story that each insect has its own regular rhythm and merely adjusts its phase to match neighbours: an isolated individual does not retain the group's regular burst periodicity. It produces sporadic bursts with highly variable intervals. When the number of fireflies in a visually connected group increases, collective periodicity emerges; in the reported enclosure experiments it became apparent at around 15 individuals.

This suggests that group-level timing can emerge from interactions among individuals even when individual burst timing is irregular. The 2022 study's model explains this emergence statistically from the distribution of individual inter-burst intervals and group size; it is not simply evidence that each firefly carries a precise metronome. This result is specific to the studied species and conditions. Other species may use different signalling and synchronisation mechanisms.

## What the comparison shows
The conventional account already explains that synchrony is a group-level phenomenon involving visual interactions. MP makes the contrast between individual and collective behaviour, and the role of density and visibility, especially explicit. Its No Sacred States lens helps prevent the mistaken assumption that a group rhythm must be an intrinsic individual rhythm.

MP does not establish the biological mechanism or add a new one. The empirical work does that. Its contribution is to make the context dependence and levels of explanation more visible.

## Evidence and qualifications
- Sarfati et al., “Emergent periodicity in the collective synchronous flashing of fireflies,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* (2022), reports that *P. carolinus* individuals in visual isolation do not show the collective burst periodicity; periodicity emerges as group size increases, appearing around 15 individuals in their enclosure experiments. https://elifesciences.org/articles/78908 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12629593/  
- The study reports collective bursts roughly every 12 seconds, with rapid flashes within each burst. Its model explains emergence of group periodicity from the distribution of individual inter-burst intervals and group size. This is not a universal account of all firefly synchrony.
- A 2024 study of five sympatric firefly species documents substantial variability in flash pattern parameters and notes that synchrony is known only in some groups/species, underscoring the importance of species-specific signalling. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53671-3
- A review of synchronously flashing fireflies describes visually coordinated inter-individual behaviour and diverse communication systems. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3059390/

## Finding
**Supported:** In *P. carolinus*, the regular group burst rhythm is an emergent collective phenomenon; isolated individuals do not exhibit the same periodicity under the reported conditions.  
**Supported with qualifications:** Visual interactions and group size matter in the studied case.  
**Not supported:** All fireflies synchronise in the same way, or every individual carries a regular rhythm that is simply phase-locked to its neighbours.  
**MP contribution:** Makes the individual/group distinction and context dependence inspectable. No new biological mechanism or demonstrated answer-quality gain.
