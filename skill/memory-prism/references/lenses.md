# The seven lenses, as the essays actually state them

Read the entry for a lens only when you are considering it. Each entry summarises what the essay claims (so you do not invent claims for it), says when it adds value, and names the trap. Open the essay in `source-corpus/` when exact wording matters.

Lenses are optional and carry no quota or cap. A lens earns its place only if it changes what you notice, infer, research or ask next. None of them replaces a core Framework concept (see `concepts.md`).

## Foundation essays

### Boards — `source-corpus/foundations/Boards.md`
- **Claims (a more structural elaboration of the Framework definition, which stays canonical):** A board is a persistent structured configuration under a dominant constraint regime, with internal state-change thresholds. Boards nest and dominance flows downward. Boards differ by six coupled variables: **budget, transport capacity, scale, sparsity, disturbance regime, turnover rate**. Named board types: sparse/dense, archive (renewal slower than residue accumulation), gradual hardening, resizing (maintenance-to-intake ratio), exit boards (erratic vs planned), cosmic.
- **Key distinction:** *internal failure* (thresholds exceeded under unchanged dominant constraints) versus *external override* (a higher board shifts the regime).
- **Use when:** scale, nesting, or "did the rules change or did the system fail within the rules?" matters.
- **Trap:** using "board" to mean "context". Say which variable differs.

### Audits — `source-corpus/foundations/Audits.md`
- **Claims:** A spectrum: threshold-only systems (non-living; transition when limits are crossed, no detection, no correction) → systems with detection and correction → systems with foresight. A **viability envelope** needs four things: a boundary, active maintenance of internal gradients, detection of deviation, and correction sustained by energy flow. **Audit** is the mapping of system state onto that envelope. Central dynamic: **disturbance rate versus correction rate**; lag compresses the viable range. Also covers tolerance distributions, elasticity and accumulating microdamage, normalisation of harm (continuous surveillance is expensive, so slow erosion is under-watched), dormancy as mode shift, and programmed shutdown.
- **Use when:** something detects drift and corrects it, or you are tempted to say it does.
- **Trap:** calling a threshold, an external measurement, or a restored state an audit. "Rocks have thresholds. Cells have envelopes."

### Identity — `source-corpus/foundations/Identity.md`
- **Claims:** "Identity enforcement" is membership governance under finite energy in systems that hold organised gradients against decay: remove what destabilises, repair what can be salvaged, tolerate what is neutral, integrate what is beneficial, sequester what cannot be removed cheaply, escalate when destabilisation exceeds tolerance. It is cost-weighted classification, not "self versus foreign". Compatibility is a gate; enforcement modulates the outcome. It varies by region and has gateways where monitoring is thin. It is **not** intention, morality, abstract selfhood, or homeostasis (which regulates variable ranges).
- **Operational meaning (project owner):** membership and role governance: what is permitted to function, where, in what role, and how that role can change or fade. The essay grew out of questions about bacterial renters in and on the human body. Keep the name Identity.
- **Use when:** what belongs to the system, in what role and place, and what is done about what does not, is part of the puzzle.
- **Extensions:** applying it to software or groups is an explicit extension of a biological essay, not a claim about what the essay originally said. Identify the boundary; what may persist or operate within it; permitted roles and locations; resources and costs; conditions for toleration, restriction, displacement or removal; and how roles change or fade without membership necessarily ending. Do not treat presence, permission and function as interchangeable.
- **Trap:** reading it as "what makes this the same thing over time", or applying it to a pile or a passive mixture.

### Flow Under Constraint — `source-corpus/foundations/Flow-under-Constraint.md`
- **Claims:** Movement follows gradients, not geometry. Viability is a cost gradient (a route may be possible yet unsustainable). Shared constraints produce shared paths (convergence); repetition stabilises paths (memory); convergence compresses alternatives, producing chokepoints that emerge rather than being designed. Some flows cannot stop (continuity), so disruption becomes systemic. Entry into an existing flow can be enough for spread (exploitation). Position can outweigh capacity.
- **Use when:** routes, costs, chokepoints, dependencies or spread are central.
- **Trap:** collapsing it into Ledger (what is paid) or Accumulation (what builds up). Flow is about movement.

## Supplementary essays

### Working Lines — `source-corpus/supplementary/Working-Lines.md`
- **Claims:** A line is a preferred path along which force, energy or coordination moves. Lines are what make structure and function possible, and they are also where systems break: "Failure is not where things are weakest. It is where forces were already being handled." Energy and space (temperature, pressure) decide whether lines can exist and what shape they take. Lines range from temporary footpaths to costly infrastructure; the more a line is optimised for flow, the more consequential its failure. Without lines (glass-like systems) there is no guided failure, only sudden collapse. Some systems place their breakpoints deliberately (fuses, break planes). Lines also store the tension they hold back, and systems can change by rewriting lines instead of breaking them.
- **Use when:** you are asking why something failed where it did, or where stress will concentrate.
- **Trap:** reading working lines as simply "existing weaknesses". The essay starts from function and treats failure as the same lines used in reverse.

### No Sacred States — `source-corpus/supplementary/No-Sacred-States.md`
- **Claims:** Material behaviour is not intrinsic; it is the board-specific outcome of what dominates there. Same carbon, graphite or diamond; same CO₂, dissolved or escaping. There are universal laws and board-dependent expressions. "Locked" only means no available editor is strong enough on the current board. Recognition is also board-dependent: people recognise behaviour on a familiar board; instruments extend recognition across boards but remain mediated.
- **Use when:** an assumption about "how X behaves" is being carried from one board to another.
- **Trap:** using it to excuse not checking the actual mechanism on the new board.

### Accumulation Patterns — `source-corpus/supplementary/Accumulation-Patterns.md`
- **Claims:** Contact is not enough. An accumulation holds only with **cohesion** (elements prefer contact), **connectivity** (percolation: local contacts chain into a spanning network, with a sudden transition), and recognition that **a subset carries the load** (force chains). Where stress spreads across a field instead (fabrics, tissue, foam), **filler** does structural work as deformable cushioning. Every part has an ongoing cost, so parts are trimmed, tolerated or preserved by cost versus contribution, and real systems settle at a minimum viable presence. The essay states explicitly that this is *not* about flow: it asks why there is a structure to work at all.
- **Use when:** pile versus structure, what is load-bearing, why something is cheaper to keep than remove.
- **Trap:** treating proximity as cohesion, or connection as equal load-sharing.

## Neighbouring lenses, compactly

| Question | Lens |
|---|---|
| Why is there a structure here at all? | Accumulation Patterns |
| How do force, energy or coordination move through it, and where does it break? | Working Lines |
| What moves, at what cost, along which routes? | Flow Under Constraint |
| What is paid, held, or owed to keep it intact? | Ledger (core concept, not a lens) |
| What does the system keep, tolerate or remove? | Identity |
| Does anything detect and correct drift? | Audits |
| Did the rules change, or did it fail within them? | Boards |
| Is the behaviour intrinsic, or board-dependent? | No Sacred States |
