# Framework concept map

Read this when you are mapping a situation onto Memory Prism concepts, or when a distinction (constraint vs editor, ledger vs flow, audit vs threshold) matters. It points to where each concept lives in the Framework and names the usual ways it gets misused. It does **not** replace the Framework text. For exact wording, open `source-corpus/Memory-Prism-Framework.md` and search for the section named in the second column.

The Framework (v1.0) has these sections, in order: Memory · Constraints · Editors · Board · Ledger · Viability Band and Correction Window · Elasticity, Inertia and Lag · Feedback loops · Dead, alive, maybe-dead and never alive · Still standing · Still stable, but for how long? · Conclusion.

## Core concepts

| Concept | Framework section | Core claim | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Memory (P-Memory)** | Memory: past's etchings | Persistent structure shaped by past interactions. Structural, not cognitive. Exists whether or not anyone reads it. | Reading it as recollection, stored data, a record, or an intended deliverable. Treating persistence as permanence. |
| **Translator** | Memory; Board | Any system able to extract differentiation from structure. Meaning arises at the interface between structure and translator. Translators are board-dependent, costly, and can over- or under-interpret. | Calling every sensor, threshold or measurement a translator, or assuming a translator exists where nothing differentiates or responds. |
| **Constraints** | Constraints | Passive bounds on what is possible (physical law or stabilised legacy). They define what cannot occur; they do not act, choose or filter. | Treating a constraint as an actor, or as a cause of which viable option survives. |
| **Editors** | Editors | Active filters that differentially shape how long viable configurations persist. By effect, not intent. Editing is differential persistence in motion. | Inventing an editor because something changed. Requiring an agent. Confusing editors with constraints. |
| **Legacy** | Editors | Stabilised memory that outlives its editor and can become a constraint: Constraint → Editor → Memory → Legacy → Constraint. | Assuming legacy needs its original editor to still be present. "Legacy outlives agency." |
| **Board** | Let the board games begin | A time- and scale-specific survival environment composed of constraints and editors, active and inherited. Boards nest; the dominant scale prevails; micro-boards do not escape macro-constraints. | Using "board" as a vague synonym for context. Ignoring which scale and timescale the question is on. |
| **Ledger** | The ledger | Energy in, energy out, and the minimum payments needed to keep structure intact. Survival is the continuous ability to meet those payments, not accumulation. | Demoting it to an optional lens. Reducing it to money. Collapsing it into Flow or Accumulation. |
| **Viability band / correction window** | Viability Band and Correction Window | Viability is funded tolerance, magnitude-bound and time-bound. The correction window is the finite time in which a perturbation can still be reversed. | Treating viability as a fixed property rather than something paid for. |
| **Elasticity, inertia, lag** | Elasticity, Inertia, and Lag | Elastic systems redistribute load; rigid ones concentrate stress and fail abruptly. Lag is often accumulated stress masked by inertia. | Reading a delay as safety. |
| **Constraint assertion** | Feedback loops | What is called feedback is enforcement triggered by overextension. Audit and checkpoint mechanisms are structural manifestations of it. | Assuming correction is immediate; in sticky boards imbalance accumulates quietly before abrupt release. |
| **Living matter** | Dead, alive, maybe-dead, and never alive | Matter that must continuously acquire, manage and account for energy to hold organisation against decay. Dormancy defers payment; it does not close the account. Fossils are closed ledgers. | Treating dormant as dead, or any energy-consuming thing as alive. |
| **Architecture / Stability** | Still standing; Still stable, but for how long? | Architecture is the committed configuration; stability is how long it holds. Stability is time-bound, conditional, scale-dependent and diagnosed retrospectively. Stability can be confinement of change, not absence of it. | Treating a stable-looking state as intrinsically stable. |

## Keep these three distinct

- **Ledger** accounts for availability, reserves, expenditure and minimum requirements.
- **Flow Under Constraint** (essay) describes movement along paths and gradients.
- **Accumulation Patterns** (essay) asks what builds up into connected, load-bearing structure, and why it is there at all.

Constraints bound what is possible. Editors shape which viable options last. A model that merges the two has lost the Framework's central distinction.

## Making the Ledger concrete

Name what is actually available, acquired, consumed, stored and owed, and what happens when the minimum payments cannot be met. Examples:

- **Biology:** stored reserves, maintenance and repair cost, cost of dormancy, resources needed to restart.
- **Materials/geology:** energy budget, stored strain, the energy a process can still draw on.
- **Software and operations:** compute, storage, I/O, connection capacity, lock duration, timeout and retry budgets, operator time, rollback capacity, dry-run cost.

If nothing concrete can be named, the ledger probably does not belong in that answer.

## Two board definitions, two levels

Both are kept. Neither replaces the other.

- **Framework (canonical):** a board is a time- and scale-specific survival environment governed by constraints and editors.
- **Boards essay (analytical elaboration):** a persistent, structured configuration of matter or energy operating under a dominant constraint regime, with internal thresholds and state transitions, characterised by budget, transport capacity, scale, sparsity, disturbance regime and turnover rate.

Use the Framework definition for what a board is. Use the essay's structure and variables to analyse and compare boards. Do not force them into identical wording; say which level you are using if it matters.

## P-Memory

P-Memory is a retrieval-friendly label for the Framework's Memory concept. It adds nothing and renames nothing. See `user-orientation.md` for how the user states it.
