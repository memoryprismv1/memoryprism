# Memory Prism Starter Kit — Checklist v1.1

**Portable starter kit for entering unfamiliar topics. Works with a person, a search engine, an expert, or an AI assistant.**

## Purpose

Help a person enter an unfamiliar topic and find a productive first route of inquiry.

This is a starter kit, not an exhaustive audit, a substitute for domain knowledge, a complete answer, or a wiki generator. It should speed up inquiry without prescribing the route or taking curiosity away from the person.

The checklist has done its job when it helps identify a useful next move. Completion is not the goal.

## Source boundary

Derived from the **Memory Prism Framework** and exactly these seven supporting essays:

| Foundation | Supplementary |
|---|---|
| Boards | Working Lines |

Other Memory Prism essays are not required material. Do not assume every topic contains every concept.

## Framework terms used below

These come from the Framework itself, not from this checklist. The Framework text is authoritative.

| Term | Meaning in the Framework |
|---|---|
| **Memory** (retrieval label: **P-Memory**, Persistent Memory) | Persistent structure shaped by past interactions. Structural, not cognitive; no storage, recall, intent or recognition required. |
| **Constraints** | Passive bounds on which configurations are possible (physical law or stabilised legacy). They do not act or choose. |
| **Editors** | Active filters that differentially shape how long viable configurations persist. They act by effect, not intent. |
| **Board** | A time- and scale-specific survival environment composed of constraints and editors. Boards nest; the dominant scale prevails. |
| **Translator** | Any system able to extract differentiation from structure (a receptor, an instrument, an audit process). Translators are themselves subject to constraints and cost. |
| **Legacy** | Stabilised memory that outlives the editor that made it, and can become a constraint. |
| **Ledger** | Energy accounting: energy in, energy out, and the minimum payments required to keep structure intact. |
| **Viability band / correction window** | The funded range of disturbance a structure can absorb, and the finite time in which a disturbance can still be reversed. |
| **Elasticity, inertia, lag** | Elastic systems redistribute load; rigid ones concentrate stress. Lag is often stored stress, not safety. |
| **Constraint assertion** | What is called "feedback": enforcement triggered when a system overextends what its board can fund or absorb. |
| **Living matter** | Matter that must continuously acquire, manage and account for energy to hold organisation against decay. Dormancy defers payment; it does not close the account. |
| **Architecture / Stability** | The committed configuration that exists / how long it holds (time-bound, conditional, scale-dependent). |

Ledger is part of the original Framework. It is not an add-on of this checklist, and it is not one of the seven optional essay lenses.

## The starter checklist

Use these as a first pass. Do not answer every question automatically. Skip anything that adds no explanatory value.

### 1. What am I actually trying to understand?

- What is the observed thing, event, pattern or puzzle?
- What needs explaining: its existence, behaviour, persistence, spread, change or failure?
- What unit and timescale make the question tractable?

*When mapping the Memory concept, use **P-Memory**: persistent structure shaped by past interactions, including naturally occurring physical structures. An intended goal or deliverable is one case, not a prerequisite. Persistent does not mean permanent.*

### 2. What is the board?

- What conditions and constraints shape what can happen here? What sets the limits: space, time, physical law, resources, budgets, learned conventions, institutional rules?
- What processes or pressures filter outcomes within those limits? (Gravity constrains what can stay supported; selection, competition, enforcement or scarcity make some configurations less likely to persist.)
- Are there nested boards, local micro-conditions, or higher-level conditions that change what is possible?
- *Optional, from the Boards essay:* how do budget, transport capacity, scale, sparsity, disturbance regime and turnover rate differ here?

A board is not a backdrop. Distinguish the conditions that set possibilities from the processes that filter outcomes, without treating the distinction as a vocabulary exercise.

Examples: a corner of a room can differ from the room as a whole; a learned convention can shape traffic or interface behaviour; a device at 40% charge is in a different state from one at 100% without necessarily being on a different board.

### 3. What moves, accumulates, and costs something?

**3a. Flow and accumulation**
- What is transferred or changed: matter, energy, information, people, pressure, money, effort?
- What gradients, costs, paths, bottlenecks or barriers shape that movement?
- What gets concentrated, repeated, stored, dissipated, removed or left behind?
- Which of these are inputs or expenditures that belong in the ledger, not just movements to describe?

**3b. The ledger**
- What energy and other resources are available now? What is held in reserve, and what must be acquired continuously?
- What are the minimum payments needed to keep the structure intact (operation, maintenance, repair, growth, recovery)?
- What happens when inflow falls below those payments? What is reduced, shed or allowed to fail first?
- If activity is paused (dormancy, standby, a frozen process), is the account closed or only deferred?

Use the ledger wherever resource availability, reserves or maintenance costs bear on persistence. Make it concrete (what is actually consumed, stored, owed) and do not reduce it to a money metaphor.

### 4. What makes the arrangement persist?

Keep these three available. Use only the ones that fit.

**4a. Pile check**
- Is this a connected structure, or merely things near one another?
- What distinguishes a pile, a cluster, and a structure that holds together? (Accumulation Patterns: cohesion, connectivity, and which subset actually carries load.)

**4b. Drift and maintenance**
- What happens if the arrangement is left alone? How fast does it drift, degrade or disperse?
- What recurring work, energy or input restores or maintains it?
- What is cheaper to include, tolerate, repair, replace or remove?

**4c. Identity (membership and role governance)**
- What is the relevant boundary, and what may persist or operate within it?
- What roles and locations are permitted? What resources and costs are involved?
- Under what conditions is something tolerated, restricted, displaced or removed? How can a role change or fade without membership ending?

Do not treat presence, permission and function as interchangeable: something can remain present while losing a role, or be permitted in one region and excluded from another. In the Identity essay this is governance, not selfhood or sameness over time. The essay is biological; applying it to software, groups or conventions is an explicit extension, justified only where there is a boundary, finite resources and a conditional response. Do not force it onto a pile or process that does not need it.

### 5. Where does it change, get interpreted, corrected, or fail?

- What thresholds or conditions change its state?
- Does any part of the system detect or interpret signals? If so, what reads them, what can it detect, at what cost, and what happens if a signal is missed or over-read?
- If the system actively maintains itself, what detects deviation and what corrects it? Is disturbance arriving faster than correction can keep up? What is delayed, unaffordable or beyond the correction window?
- Is apparent stability real, or is change being confined or delayed (stored stress, suppressed mixing, lag)?
- Which paths enable normal function, and could those same paths carry stress or failure?
- What did earlier events leave behind that changes the present?
- Is this a failure within unchanged constraints, or did a higher-level board change the rules?

Do not assume every system detects, interprets, corrects, anticipates or governs anything. Some systems simply undergo physical transitions. A "translator" is relevant only where something actually extracts differentiation from structure and responds to it. A threshold crossed is not automatically an audit.

### 6. What is the best next question?

- What remains uncertain?
- What are two plausible explanations that lead to different expectations?
- What observation, source, comparison or small test could tell them apart? What would we expect to find under each?

Prefer a next question that can change what we believe or do. If a settled answer exists, give it first; use the checklist only for the genuinely open part. Stop when you have a useful direction.

## Seven optional lenses

A library of ways into a problem, not seven stages. Use none, one or several, depending on what each changes about the inquiry. There is no quota and no cap: do not apply all seven by default, and do not drop a useful lens to hit a number. Often one or two earn their place.

| Lens | Entry question |
|---|---|
| **Boards** | Which constraints and configurations make this possible? Where are the nested boards, and what changes when a higher board shifts? |
| **Audits** | Is anything detecting deviation against a viability envelope and correcting it, or does the system simply cross a threshold? Is disturbance outpacing correction? |
| **Identity** | Who or what may operate within the boundary, in which roles and places, at what cost, and how are roles tolerated, restricted, displaced, removed or allowed to fade? |
| **Flow Under Constraint** | What moves along which gradients or routes, at what cost? Which flows cannot stop, and where do paths converge into chokepoints? |
| **Working Lines** | Which pathways carry force, energy or coordination, and how might those same pathways carry stress or failure? Where were its lines? |
| **No Sacred States** | Are we treating a behaviour or state as intrinsic when it depends on the board it sits on? |
| **Accumulation Patterns** | What turns contact or repeated presence into connected, load-bearing structure? Which parts carry load, which are cushioning or filler, and why is keeping it cheaper than dismantling it? |

After using a lens, ask: **did it earn its keep?** If it did not change what you noticed, inferred, researched or asked next, leave it out. Similar-looking patterns are leads, not proof of the same mechanism.

## Operating rules for AI assistants

1. **Start with the user's observation.** Do not make the user fill out the checklist.
2. **Use it lightly.** Usually surface only one to three structurally useful observations.
3. **Keep curiosity open.** Offer a few promising domain-specific next moves, not an exhaustive map.
4. **Do the next obvious piece of work yourself.** Research, compare, inspect, calculate or test. Do not make the user supply the next research step or supervise your reasoning.
5. **Distinguish evidence from interpretation when it matters**, using these labels where useful:
   - **[Fact]** supported by a source or direct observation.
   - **[Reasonable inference]** plausible from available evidence but not established.
   - **[Speculation]** a possible explanation that needs testing.
   - **[Open question]** a material uncertainty still unresolved.
6. **Do not overfit.** Similar structures are leads, not proof of a shared mechanism.
7. **Do not anthropomorphise.** Avoid implying intention, detection, correction or governance without a supporting mechanism.
8. **Do not manufacture a complete analysis** because the framework can generate one. Stop before furnishing every room.
9. **Ask for clarification only when missing information blocks progress.** Otherwise state a reasonable assumption and proceed.
10. **Check whether each lens earned its keep.** Remove it if it adds no explanatory value.
11. **Let domain mechanisms lead.** Use ordinary domain language in the answer. MP helps find an entry point; it does not decide the answer in advance.
12. **Answer settled parts directly.** Do not withhold a straightforward answer to perform a framework exercise.

### Assistant reasoning audit (AI-only)

Run internally before finalising substantive exploratory work. This is not a Memory Prism concept or a questionnaire for the user.

- What assumptions did I inherit?
- What information did I use, or fail to use?
- What constraints shaped the answer?
- Which editor favoured this particular response?
- Where did the reasoning take a shortcut?
- What alternative structures could have produced a better result?

Use the answers to revise the work, not to recite them. Disclose material assumptions, omissions or shortcuts when they affect the result. Do not invent an editor where none is established. Not required for simple settled answers.

### Suggested response shape

Keep the answer proportionate. A useful default:

- **What seems structurally important:** one to three observations, evidence separated from inference.
- **What remains uncertain:** one important uncertainty or competing explanation.
- **Where to go next:** one to three questions, searches, comparisons or tests, including what would distinguish competing explanations.

A simple topic may need only a direct answer.
